This section reports the outcome of the experimental blocks of
Section~\ref{sec:methods}, in the order of the research questions of
Section~\ref{sec:proposed_approach}: the three-contributor decomposition
(Section~\ref{subsec:res_decomp}, RQ5), the $\lambda$-curriculum sweep on
rot-MNIST (Section~\ref{subsec:res_curriculum}, RQ1, RQ2, RQ4), the momentum
cross (Section~\ref{subsec:res_momentum}, RQ3), the CIFAR-10 generalisation
block (Section~\ref{subsec:res_cifar}, RQ6), and the long-sequence rot-MNIST check
(Section~\ref{subsec:res_longseq}).

\paragraph{How to read the numbers.} The headline metric is \texttt{gap\_depth},
the worst single-step drop in past-task accuracy at the transition
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:gap_metrics}). We report it in percentage points (pp),
so a depth of $19.5$ means the worst evaluation point sat $19.5$ accuracy
points below the pre-task baseline. \texttt{gap\_area} (depth $\times$ duration)
and the continual-learning metrics of Table~\ref{tab:cl_metrics} are reported as
fractions. Every entry is the mean over five seeds (three for the CIFAR
generalisation block) with the seed standard deviation. With five paired seeds
the smallest two-sided Wilcoxon $p$-value attainable is $0.0625$, reached when
all five seed-paired deltas share a sign; we therefore read ``$p = 0.0625$'' as
``all five seeds agree in direction'' and lean on effect sizes and seed-paired
consistency rather than a $0.05$ threshold the design cannot reach. The full
metric tables for every condition are in Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}.

\subsection{Three-Contributor Decomposition (RQ5)}
\label{subsec:res_decomp}

Table~\ref{tab:res_decomp} reports gap depth and final accuracy along the
removal path G1 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4 of
Section~\ref{subsec:decomp}, with the full-data control G2 and the NCL
path-finding reference. Reading the depth column against the differencing rules
of Section~\ref{subsec:decomp} gives the three contributor shares.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{lcc}
        \hline
        Condition & \texttt{gap\_depth} (pp) & ACC \\
        \hline
        G1 --- Vanilla ER          & $19.5 \pm 4.1$ & $0.873 \pm 0.005$ \\
        G2 --- Full-data ER        & $18.3 \pm 2.5$ & $0.890 \pm 0.009$ \\
        G3 --- Balanced ER         & $5.7 \pm 1.9$  & $0.849 \pm 0.005$ \\
        G4 --- Full-data balanced  & $1.9 \pm 1.3$  & $0.869 \pm 0.002$ \\
        NCL (reference)            & $7.2 \pm 1.0$  & $0.770 \pm 0.012$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Decomposition conditions on rot-MNIST (momentum $0.0$). Depth falls
    monotonically along G1 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4. NCL attains a low
    depth but at a large accuracy cost.}
    \label{tab:res_decomp}
\end{table}

The differencing of Section~\ref{subsec:decomp} yields
\begin{align*}
    G_{\text{mag}}  &= 19.5 - 5.7 = 13.8 \text{ pp} \quad (71\%), \\
    G_{\text{est}}  &= 5.7 - 1.9 = 3.8 \text{ pp} \quad (19\%), \\
    G_{\text{traj}} &= 1.9 \text{ pp} \quad (10\%).
\end{align*}
The magnitude asymmetry is the dominant contributor: removing it by unit-norm
balancing (G3) erases roughly two-thirds of the gap on its own. The estimator
and trajectory shares are each small. The orthogonal sanity check is only
partially consistent --- the unbalanced full-data step removes
$\texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G1}) - \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G2}) = 1.2$ pp,
against the $3.8$ pp removed by full data on top of balancing. Both estimates
place the estimator contribution in the low single-digit-pp range; the
difference indicates that estimator noise and magnitude asymmetry are not
perfectly separable, which is expected since a noisy replay gradient also
inflates the first-step magnitude. The trajectory residual at G4 is $1.9$ pp ---
small but non-zero, matching the prediction of
Section~\ref{subsec:stability_gap} and Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}, and exactly the
share the $\lambda$-curriculum is built to suppress.

The accuracy column already shows the cost structure that motivates the
curriculum. Balancing (G3) reduces depth but pays $2.4$ pp of final accuracy
relative to vanilla ER; NCL reaches a comparable depth ($7.2$ pp) but sacrifices
$10$ pp of accuracy. A method that removes the trajectory residual without an
accuracy cost is what Sections~\ref{subsec:res_curriculum}
and~\ref{subsec:res_momentum} test.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda}-Curriculum on rot-MNIST (RQ1, RQ2, RQ4)}
\label{subsec:res_curriculum}

Table~\ref{tab:res_curriculum} reports the full curriculum sweep at both
momentum settings, against the vanilla-ER baseline (G1). We describe the
momentum-off leg first, then the momentum-on leg, which is the headline regime.

\begin{table*}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{l cc cc}
        \hline
        & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Momentum $0.0$} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Momentum $0.9$} \\
        Condition & \texttt{gap\_depth} (pp) & ACC & \texttt{gap\_depth} (pp) & ACC \\
        \hline
        Vanilla ER (baseline)               & $19.5 \pm 4.1$ & $0.873 \pm 0.005$ & $15.9 \pm 2.6$ & $0.928 \pm 0.003$ \\
        Linear $N{=}50$                     & $17.5 \pm 1.7$ & $0.851 \pm 0.023$ & $6.4 \pm 0.5$  & $0.932 \pm 0.003$ \\
        Linear $N{=}100$                    & $16.2 \pm 4.3$ & $0.844 \pm 0.025$ & $6.4 \pm 0.6$  & $0.932 \pm 0.003$ \\
        Linear $N{=}200$                    & $14.3 \pm 4.6$ & $0.828 \pm 0.025$ & $6.2 \pm 0.6$  & $0.925 \pm 0.007$ \\
        Adaptive                            & $12.8 \pm 2.9$ & $0.833 \pm 0.025$ & $2.5 \pm 0.5$  & $0.917 \pm 0.001$ \\

        Adaptive $+\,\lambda_{\min}{=}0.20$ & $13.9 \pm 3.4$ & $0.837 \pm 0.031$ & $3.7 \pm 0.4$  & $0.931 \pm 0.002$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{$\lambda$-curriculum sweep on rot-MNIST against the vanilla-ER
    baseline, at momentum $0.0$ and $0.9$. Depth in percentage points, ACC as a
    fraction; mean $\pm$ std over five seeds.}
    \label{tab:res_curriculum}
\end{table*}

\paragraph{Linear curriculum (RQ1).} At momentum $0.0$, gap depth falls
monotonically as the ramp lengthens --- $19.5 \rightarrow 17.5 \rightarrow 16.2
\rightarrow 14.3$ pp from vanilla through $N = 200$ --- consistent with the
$O(1/N)$ bound of Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}. The reduction is modest in absolute
terms over this range of $N$, and it comes with a small accuracy drift: ACC
falls from $0.873$ (vanilla) to $0.828$ at $N = 200$, a $4.4$ pp cost with all
five seeds agreeing in direction ($p = 0.0625$). So at momentum $0.0$ the
prediction that the linear curriculum matches vanilla accuracy is only partly
borne out --- the curriculum trades a little accuracy for depth in this regime.
Figure~\ref{fig:linear_scaling} plots depth against $N$ for the $O(1/N)$ check.

\paragraph{Adaptive curriculum (RQ2).} At momentum $0.0$ the adaptive schedule
reaches $12.8$ pp, below the best linear curriculum ($14.3$ pp at $N = 200$) and
without an explicit ramp length $N$. The self-paced schedule therefore removes
the knob $N$ while at least matching the best hand-tuned linear ramp, confirming
RQ2. Its accuracy ($0.833$) carries the same small drift as the linear runs at
this momentum setting.

\paragraph{The $\lambda_{\min}$ floor (RQ4).} The $\lambda_{\min}$ effect is
clearest in the momentum-on leg (right half of Table~\ref{tab:res_curriculum}),
where the gap is small enough to see the trade. Raising $\lambda_{\min}$ from
$0$ to $0.20$ lifts gap depth slightly ($2.5 \rightarrow 3.7$ pp) while raising
final accuracy ($0.917 \rightarrow 0.931$) and short-horizon plasticity
($\mathrm{WP}_{100}: 0.798 \rightarrow 0.812$; Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}).
This is the predicted Pareto trade: a floor on $\lambda$ restores early-task
learning speed at the cost of a small, controlled increase in depth.
$\lambda_{\min} = 0.20$ gives the best final accuracy and matches vanilla ER's
accuracy at momentum $0.9$ ($0.931$ vs $0.928$) while cutting depth from $15.9$
to $3.7$ pp; it is the setting carried forward to CIFAR
(Section~\ref{subsec:res_cifar}). Figure~\ref{fig:lambdamin_pareto} shows the
(depth, ACC) front.

\subsection{Momentum Cross (RQ3)}
\label{subsec:res_momentum}

Two predictions sit behind RQ3: momentum on top of the curriculum should
suppress the residual stochastic oscillation, and momentum alone, without the
curriculum, should not reduce gap depth. Both hold.

\paragraph{Momentum alone does not close the gap.} For vanilla ER, momentum
moves depth from $19.5$ to $15.9$ pp, a change in which the five seeds do not
agree in direction ($p = 0.44$) and which is small next to the seed spread. The
deterministic one-sided first step at the boundary is unchanged by momentum, so
momentum alone leaves the gap essentially intact --- the falsifiable null
prediction of Section~\ref{subsec:momentum} is confirmed.

\paragraph{Momentum on top of the curriculum closes it.} Applied to the
curriculum, momentum has a large effect. The adaptive schedule drops from $12.8$
to $2.5$ pp and the linear $N = 200$ schedule from $14.3$ to $6.2$ pp, both with
all five seeds agreeing ($p = 0.0625$). The seed-to-seed standard deviation
collapses at the same time: from $2.9$ to $0.5$ pp for the adaptive schedule and
from $4.6$ to $0.6$ pp for linear $N = 200$. This is the signature predicted in
Section~\ref{subsec:momentum} --- once the curriculum has made the optimum path
continuous, the residual gap is stochastic oscillation around that path, and
momentum averages it away. The combination of a continuous schedule and momentum,
not either alone, is what drives the gap down to a few percentage points while
recovering vanilla-ER accuracy.

\subsection{Generalisation: CIFAR-10 (RQ6)}
\label{subsec:res_cifar}

The CIFAR-10 block tests whether the rot-MNIST finding carries to a deeper
backbone (ResNet-18), a stronger task shift (clean $\rightarrow$ corruption),
and the two normalisation choices that govern the post-switch recovery
(batch norm, group norm). Table~\ref{tab:res_cifar} reports the six headline
conditions; the shipped curriculum is the adaptive schedule with
$\lambda_{\min} = 0.20$.

\begin{table*}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{ll ccc}
        \hline
        Norm & Method & \texttt{gap\_depth} (pp) & ACC & min-ACC \\
        \hline
        \multirow{3}{*}{BatchNorm}
            & D1 --- Vanilla ER          & $11.8 \pm 2.5$  & $0.723 \pm 0.008$ & $0.644 \pm 0.023$ \\
            & D2 --- NCL                 & $25.8 \pm 5.2$  & $0.710 \pm 0.005$ & $0.504 \pm 0.045$ \\
            & D3 --- Adaptive $\lambda_{\min}{=}0.20$ & $\mathbf{4.8 \pm 0.7}$ & $0.722 \pm 0.011$ & $0.713 \pm 0.017$ \\
        \hline
        \multirow{3}{*}{GroupNorm}
            & D4 --- Vanilla ER          & $41.2 \pm 13.5$ & $0.730 \pm 0.011$ & $0.309 \pm 0.111$ \\
            & D5 --- NCL                 & $29.7 \pm 10.0$ & $0.711 \pm 0.017$ & $0.423 \pm 0.083$ \\
            & D6 --- Adaptive $\lambda_{\min}{=}0.20$ & $\mathbf{5.2 \pm 2.1}$ & $0.731 \pm 0.020$ & $0.670 \pm 0.055$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{CIFAR-10 headline block (ResNet-18, momentum $0.9$, buffer $5{,}000$,
    three methods $\times$ two normalisations, five seeds). The adaptive
    curriculum cuts gap depth far below both vanilla ER and NCL, at matched final
    accuracy and much higher worst-case retention (min-ACC).}
    \label{tab:res_cifar}
\end{table*}

The rot-MNIST result carries over and strengthens. Under batch norm the adaptive
curriculum cuts depth from $11.8$ pp (vanilla) to $4.8$ pp; under group norm,
where vanilla ER suffers a catastrophic $41.2$ pp drop, it cuts depth to $5.2$
pp. In both cases final accuracy is unchanged ($\le 1$ pp difference from
vanilla) while worst-case retention improves sharply --- min-ACC rises from
$0.309$ to $0.670$ under group norm. NCL does not transfer: on this deeper
backbone its path-finding preconditioner makes the gap \emph{larger} than
vanilla ER ($25.8$ pp under batch norm), the opposite of its rot-MNIST behaviour.
This is the cleanest separation in the study between acting in schedule space
(the curriculum) and acting in parameter space (NCL): the curriculum's benefit
is robust to depth and normalisation, NCL's is not.

\paragraph{Generalisation across corruptions and sequence length.} The
generalisation block (three seeds, group-norm ship config) pairs the curriculum
against a vanilla-ER control on shifts the method was not tuned on
(Table~\ref{tab:res_cifar_gen}). On the two-task novel corruptions the curriculum
generalises cleanly: depth falls from $46.3$ to $7.3$ pp on shot noise and from
$8.4$ to $3.9$ pp on contrast. The three-task sequence is the exception --- there
the curriculum underperforms its vanilla control ($32.8$ vs $4.4$ pp, with a
large seed variance of $\pm 14.5$ pp), an anomaly that does not appear in the
batch-norm variant of the same sequence (Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}). We
read this as the adaptive schedule interacting poorly with several closely spaced
group-norm transitions rather than a single one, and flag it as a boundary of the
method's reliability rather than evidence against the two-task result; final
accuracy stays comparable to vanilla throughout.

\subsection{Long-Sequence rot-MNIST}
\label{subsec:res_longseq}

The two-task design isolates a single transition. To check that the curriculum
holds across several boundaries, we ran a five-task rot-MNIST sequence
(rotations $[0, 30, 60, 90, 120]^\circ$, four transitions) at momentum $0.9$
(Table~\ref{tab:res_longseq}).

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{l cc}
        \hline
        Condition & \texttt{gap\_depth} (pp) & ACC \\
        \hline
        L1 --- Vanilla ER                 & $8.9 \pm 1.7$ & $0.889 \pm 0.007$ \\
        L2 --- NCL                        & $\mathbf{2.5 \pm 0.6}$ & $0.815 \pm 0.003$ \\
        L3 --- Adaptive $\lambda_{\min}{=}0.00$ & $4.4 \pm 0.2$ & $0.896 \pm 0.003$ \\
        L4 --- Adaptive $\lambda_{\min}{=}0.10$ & $4.5 \pm 0.6$ & $0.896 \pm 0.003$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Five-task rot-MNIST (four transitions, momentum $0.9$). Gap depth is
    the mean worst drop across the four boundaries. The adaptive curriculum halves
    the vanilla-ER gap while preserving accuracy; NCL reaches the lowest depth but
    pays $\sim 8$ pp of accuracy.}
    \label{tab:res_longseq}
\end{table}

The adaptive curriculum carries past a single transition: it roughly halves the
vanilla-ER gap ($8.9 \rightarrow 4.4$ pp) across four boundaries while slightly
\emph{improving} final accuracy ($0.889 \rightarrow 0.896$). NCL reaches the
lowest depth of the four ($2.5$ pp) --- consistent with
\citet{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess} reporting its advantage at higher
task counts --- but at a large accuracy cost ($0.815$, $8$ pp below the
curriculum). The $\lambda_{\min}$ floor makes little difference to depth here.
The curriculum thus keeps the property that matters: a low gap at no accuracy
cost, sustained across multiple transitions.

\subsection{Summary of Findings}
\label{subsec:res_summary}

Across blocks, four results stand out. (i) On rot-MNIST the stability gap is
dominated by the first-step magnitude asymmetry ($71\%$), with a small trajectory
residual ($10\%$) that survives exact gradients and balancing --- the residual
the curriculum targets. (ii) The adaptive $\lambda$-curriculum with momentum
reduces gap depth from $15.9$ to $3.7$ pp at matched accuracy, where momentum
alone does nothing and the curriculum alone leaves a stochastic residual: the
two are complementary, as predicted. (iii) The result carries to a ResNet-18 on
CIFAR-10 under both normalisations and to novel two-task corruptions, where the
curriculum beats both vanilla ER and NCL on depth and worst-case retention at
matched accuracy. (iv) The known boundaries are an accuracy drift for the linear
curriculum at momentum $0.0$, and a high-variance failure of the adaptive
schedule on a three-task group-norm sequence. The discussion interprets these
against the theory of Sections~\ref{sec:motivation} and~\ref{sec:theory}.
