This section reports the outcome of the experimental blocks of
Section~\ref{sec:methods}, in the order of the research questions of
Section~\ref{sec:proposed_approach}: the three-contributor decomposition
(Section~\ref{subsec:res_decomp}, RQ1), the $\lambda$-curriculum sweep on
rot-MNIST (Section~\ref{subsec:res_curriculum}, RQ2a, RQ2b, RQ2c), the momentum
cross (Section~\ref{subsec:res_momentum}, RQ2d), the CIFAR-10 generalisation
block (Section~\ref{subsec:res_cifar}, RQ3), and the long-sequence rot-MNIST check
(Section~\ref{subsec:res_longseq}).

\paragraph{How to read the numbers.} The headline metric is \texttt{gap\_depth},
the worst single-step drop in past-task accuracy at the transition
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:gap_metrics}), in percentage points (pp): a depth of
$19.5$ means the worst evaluation point sat $19.5$ points below the pre-task
baseline. Depth captures only how far the dip falls. It says nothing about how
much accuracy the gap costs before the curve reconverges --- which
\texttt{gap\_area} measures: the dip integrated against the level each task
recovers to, so a permanent drop to a lower plateau does not count
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:gap_metrics}), with \texttt{recovery\_steps} timing the
return to within $90\%$ of the pre-task baseline. Depth and area are largely
independent: a deep dip that snaps back in a few steps has a large depth but a
small area, while a transient that stays open costs a large area at any depth.
The transient also has a \emph{shape} that no scalar captures --- a sharp
magnitude-driven notch or a gradual trajectory-driven bend --- so we read the
accuracy curves alongside the numbers; permanent retention loss, in turn, shows up
in ACC/FORG rather than in either depth or area. We quote area and recovery
alongside depth where they change the reading. The tables here report depth and ACC, with area where it carries the
argument; the full metric set is in Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}, and the
continual-learning metrics of Table~\ref{tab:cl_metrics} are reported as
fractions. Every entry is the mean over five seeds (three for the CIFAR
generalisation block) with the seed standard deviation. With five paired seeds
the smallest two-sided Wilcoxon $p$-value attainable is $0.0625$, reached when
all five seed-paired deltas share a sign; we therefore read ``$p = 0.0625$'' as
``all five seeds agree in direction'' and lean on effect sizes and seed-paired
consistency rather than a $0.05$ threshold the design cannot reach. The full
metric tables for every condition are in Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}.

\subsection{Three-Contributor Decomposition (RQ1)}
\label{subsec:res_decomp}

Table~\ref{tab:res_decomp} reports gap depth, area, and final accuracy along the
removal path G1 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4 of
Section~\ref{subsec:decomp}, with the full-data control G2 and the NCL
path-finding reference. Reading the depth column against the differencing rules
of Section~\ref{subsec:decomp} gives the three contributor shares.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{lccc}
        \hline
        Condition                 & \texttt{gap\_depth} (pp) & \texttt{gap\_area} & ACC               \\
        \hline
        G1 --- Vanilla ER         & $19.5 \pm 4.1$           & $6.3$              & $0.873 \pm 0.005$ \\
        G2 --- Full-data ER       & $18.3 \pm 2.5$           & $8.1$              & $0.890 \pm 0.009$ \\
        G3 --- Balanced ER        & $5.7 \pm 1.9$            & $1.4$              & $0.849 \pm 0.005$ \\
        G4 --- Full-data balanced & $1.9 \pm 1.3$            & $3.2$              & $0.869 \pm 0.002$ \\
        NCL (reference)           & $7.2 \pm 1.0$            & $0.3$              & $0.770 \pm 0.012$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Decomposition conditions on rot-MNIST (momentum $0.0$).
        NCL has the lowest bend ($0.3$) despite its low
        depth and worst accuracy --- its gap is permanent forgetting, not a
        transient; the deep vanilla spike ($19.5$ pp) leaves only a modest bend
        ($6.3$) because rot-MNIST reconverges within a few steps.}
    \label{tab:res_decomp}
\end{table}

The differencing of Section~\ref{subsec:decomp} yields
\begin{align*}
    G_{\text{mag}}  & = 19.5 - 5.7 = 13.8 \text{ pp} \quad (71\%), \\
    G_{\text{est}}  & = 5.7 - 1.9 = 3.8 \text{ pp} \quad (19\%),   \\
    G_{\text{traj}} & = 1.9 \text{ pp} \quad (10\%).
\end{align*}
The magnitude asymmetry is the dominant contributor: removing it by unit-norm
balancing (G3) erases roughly two-thirds of the gap on its own. The estimator
and trajectory shares are each small. The orthogonal sanity check is only
partially consistent --- the unbalanced full-data step removes
$\texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G1}) - \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G2}) = 1.2$ pp,
against the $3.8$ pp removed by full data on top of balancing. Both estimates
place the estimator contribution in the low single-digit-pp range; the
difference indicates that estimator noise and magnitude asymmetry are not
perfectly separable, which is expected since a noisy replay gradient also
inflates the first-step magnitude. The trajectory residual at G4 is $1.9$ pp ---
small but non-zero, matching the prediction of
Section~\ref{subsec:stability_gap} and Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}, and exactly the
share the $\lambda$-curriculum is built to suppress.

The accuracy column already shows the cost structure that motivates the
curriculum. Balancing (G3) reduces depth but pays $2.4$ pp of final accuracy
relative to vanilla ER; NCL reaches a comparable depth ($7.2$ pp) but sacrifices
$10$ pp of accuracy. A method that removes the trajectory residual without an
accuracy cost is what Sections~\ref{subsec:res_curriculum}
and~\ref{subsec:res_momentum} test.

\paragraph{The bend, separated from forgetting.} Depth measures the sharp notch
at the boundary; \texttt{gap\_area} measures the gradual \emph{bend} that follows
--- the dip below the level each task recovers to
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:gap_metrics}), so a permanent drop to a lower plateau does
not count. Figure~\ref{fig:decomp_curves} shows the bend directly. A gradual
dip-and-recover is visible in every condition except NCL: cleanest in the
full-data runs (G2, G4), whose exact replay gradients give a smooth curve without
buffer jitter, still present under vanilla ER on top of its sharp notch, and
reduced to a faint sag after balancing (G3). G4 is the diagnostic case --- with
the magnitude spike removed its depth is only $1.9$ pp, yet a bend remains (area
$3.2$): the trajectory residual showing up as a hanging curve rather than a spike,
exactly as Section~\ref{subsec:stability_gap} predicts. The areas rank the bends
as the eye does --- G2 $>$ G1 $>$ G4 $>$ G3 ($8.1, 6.3, 3.2, 1.4$). NCL is the
lone exception, and the reason we reference the recovered level rather than the
pre-task accuracy: a pre-referenced shortfall would call NCL the worst condition,
since it sits roughly $10$ pp below its starting accuracy for the rest of the
task, but that shortfall is permanent forgetting --- the same $10$ pp it pays in
final accuracy, which ACC and FORG already carry. It steps to a lower plateau and
stays, with no reconvergence to integrate, so its bend is the smallest in the
block ($0.3$). These bends are small in absolute terms because rot-MNIST
reconverges within a few steps; the deeper CIFAR backbone is where the transient
stays open long enough to dominate the gap (Section~\ref{subsec:res_cifar}).

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda}-Curriculum on rot-MNIST (RQ2a, RQ2b, RQ2c)}
\label{subsec:res_curriculum}

momentum settings, against the vanilla-ER baseline (G1). We describe the
momentum-off leg first, then the momentum-on leg, which is the headline regime.

\paragraph{Linear curriculum (RQ2a).} At momentum $0.0$, gap depth falls
monotonically as the ramp lengthens --- $19.5 \rightarrow 17.5 \rightarrow 16.2
    \rightarrow 14.3$ pp from vanilla through $N = 200$ --- consistent with the
$O(1/N)$ bound of Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}. The reduction is modest in absolute
terms over this range of $N$, and it comes with a small accuracy drift: ACC
falls from $0.873$ (vanilla) to $0.828$ at $N = 200$, a $4.4$ pp cost with all
five seeds agreeing in direction ($p = 0.0625$). So at momentum $0.0$ the
prediction that the linear curriculum matches vanilla accuracy is only partly
borne out --- the curriculum trades a little accuracy for depth in this regime. The same
lengthening that lowers depth also stretches the tail: \texttt{recovery\_steps}
grows from $36$ steps at $N=50$ to $133$ at $N=200$
(Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}), because a slower ramp holds $T_0$ accuracy
back longer even as it shrinks the spike. The transient bend still falls
($4.9 \rightarrow 1.8$ from $N=50$ to $N=200$), since the dip is shallower, but
Figure~\ref{fig:linear_curves} shows the longest ramp's curve hanging visibly
longer and noisier near baseline --- the depth gain partly trades against a
slower reconvergence.

\paragraph{Adaptive curriculum (RQ2b).} At momentum $0.0$ the adaptive schedule
reaches $12.8$ pp, below the best linear curriculum ($14.3$ pp at $N = 200$) and
without an explicit ramp length $N$. The self-paced schedule therefore removes
the knob $N$ while at least matching the best hand-tuned linear ramp, confirming
RQ2b. Its accuracy ($0.833$) carries the same small drift as the linear runs at
this momentum setting.

\paragraph{The $\lambda_{\min}$ floor (RQ2c).} At momentum $0.0$, raising
$\lambda_{\min}$ from $0$ to $0.20$ moves the adaptive curriculum in the
predicted Pareto direction but only weakly: final accuracy edges up
($0.833 \rightarrow 0.837$) and short-horizon plasticity recovers marginally
($\mathrm{WP}_{100}: 0.703 \rightarrow 0.710$; Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}),
at the cost of a slight depth increase ($12.8 \rightarrow 13.9$ pp). The effect
is small here because the momentum-off gap is dominated by the deterministic
one-sided boundary step, which a floor on $\lambda$ cannot touch. The floor
becomes decisive only once momentum suppresses that step; that regime is the
subject of Section~\ref{subsec:res_momentum} (RQ2d).

\subsection{Momentum Cross (RQ2d)}
\label{subsec:res_momentum}

Two predictions sit behind RQ2d: momentum on top of the curriculum should
suppress the residual stochastic oscillation, and momentum alone, without the
curriculum, should not reduce gap depth. Both hold.

\paragraph{Momentum alone does not close the gap.} For vanilla ER, momentum
moves depth from $19.5$ to $15.9$ pp, a change in which the five seeds do not
agree in direction ($p = 0.44$) and which is small next to the seed spread. The
deterministic one-sided first step at the boundary is unchanged by momentum, so
momentum alone leaves the gap essentially intact --- the falsifiable null
prediction of Section~\ref{subsec:momentum} is confirmed.

\paragraph{Momentum on top of the curriculum closes it.} Applied to the
curriculum, momentum has a large effect. The adaptive schedule drops from $12.8$
to $2.5$ pp and the linear $N = 200$ schedule from $14.3$ to $6.2$ pp, both with
all five seeds agreeing ($p = 0.0625$). The seed-to-seed standard deviation
collapses at the same time: from $2.9$ to $0.5$ pp for the adaptive schedule and
from $4.6$ to $0.6$ pp for linear $N = 200$. This is the signature predicted in
Section~\ref{subsec:momentum} --- once the curriculum has made the optimum path
continuous, the residual gap is stochastic oscillation around that path, and
momentum averages it away (Figure~\ref{fig:momentum_cross}). The combination of a
continuous schedule and momentum,
not either alone, is what drives the gap down to a few percentage points while
recovering vanilla-ER accuracy.

\paragraph{The $\lambda_{\min}$ floor tunes the residual trade.} With momentum
on, the floor that barely registered at momentum $0.0$ becomes a clean Pareto
knob. Its mechanism is visible on the plasticity side
(Figure~\ref{fig:lambdamin_plasticity}): a higher floor lets the new task train
from the first post-switch step, so $T_1$ accuracy climbs faster the larger
$\lambda_{\min}$ is. Raising $\lambda_{\min}$ from $0$ to
$0.20$ lifts gap depth slightly ($2.5 \rightarrow 3.7$ pp) while restoring final
accuracy ($0.917 \rightarrow 0.931$) and short-horizon plasticity
($\mathrm{WP}_{100}: 0.798 \rightarrow 0.812$; Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}).
At $\lambda_{\min} = 0.20$ the curriculum matches vanilla-ER accuracy
($0.931$ vs $0.928$) and the best linear curricula ($0.932$) while holding depth
to $3.7$ pp --- roughly half the linear curricula's $6.4$ pp and far below
vanilla's $15.9$ pp. This is the setting carried forward to CIFAR
(Section~\ref{subsec:res_cifar}).

\subsection{Generalisation: CIFAR-10 (RQ3)}
\label{subsec:res_cifar}

The CIFAR-10 block tests whether the rot-MNIST finding carries to a deeper
backbone (ResNet-18), a stronger task shift (clean $\rightarrow$ corruption),
and the two normalisation choices that govern the post-switch recovery
(batch norm, group norm). Table~\ref{tab:res_cifar} reports the six headline
conditions; the shipped curriculum is the adaptive schedule with
$\lambda_{\min} = 0.20$.

\begin{table*}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{ll cccc}
        \hline
        Norm & Method                                  & \texttt{gap\_depth} (pp) & \texttt{gap\_area} & ACC               & min-ACC           \\
        \hline
        \multirow{3}{*}{BatchNorm}
             & D1 --- Vanilla ER                       & $11.8 \pm 2.5$           & $17.5$             & $0.723 \pm 0.008$ & $0.644 \pm 0.023$ \\
             & D2 --- NCL                              & $25.8 \pm 5.2$           & $55.1$             & $0.710 \pm 0.005$ & $0.504 \pm 0.045$ \\
             & D3 --- Adaptive $\lambda_{\min}{=}0.20$ & $\mathbf{4.8 \pm 0.7}$   & $\mathbf{3.6}$     & $0.722 \pm 0.011$ & $0.713 \pm 0.017$ \\
        \hline
        \multirow{3}{*}{GroupNorm}
             & D4 --- Vanilla ER                       & $41.2 \pm 13.5$          & $34.9$             & $0.730 \pm 0.011$ & $0.309 \pm 0.111$ \\
             & D5 --- NCL                              & $29.7 \pm 10.0$          & $52.6$             & $0.711 \pm 0.017$ & $0.423 \pm 0.083$ \\
             & D6 --- Adaptive $\lambda_{\min}{=}0.20$ & $\mathbf{5.2 \pm 2.1}$   & $\mathbf{1.7}$     & $0.731 \pm 0.020$ & $0.670 \pm 0.055$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{CIFAR-10 headline block (ResNet-18, momentum $0.9$, buffer $5{,}000$,
        three methods $\times$ two normalisations, five seeds). The adaptive
        curriculum cuts both gap depth and its area (\texttt{gap\_area}, how much
        accuracy the gap costs before it recovers) far below vanilla ER and NCL, at
        matched final accuracy and much higher worst-case retention (min-ACC).}
    \label{tab:res_cifar}
\end{table*}

The rot-MNIST result carries over and strengthens. Under batch norm the adaptive
curriculum cuts depth from $11.8$ pp (vanilla) to $4.8$ pp; under group norm,
where vanilla ER suffers a catastrophic $41.2$ pp drop, it cuts depth to $5.2$
pp. In both cases final accuracy is unchanged ($\le 1$ pp difference from
vanilla) while worst-case retention improves sharply --- min-ACC rises from
$0.309$ to $0.670$ under group norm.

Here the transient is not a gentle bend but a deep stability gap that stays open
(Figure~\ref{fig:cifar_curves}): under group norm vanilla ER crashes $T_0$
accuracy from $\sim 0.73$ almost to chance and then takes several hundred steps to
recover. Because the ResNet reconverges far more slowly than the MLP, that gap
costs much more accuracy over time --- its area is large because it stays open, not
because the shape is exotic. (Area is depth $\times$ duration in steps, and the
CIFAR window is several times longer than rot-MNIST's, so we compare areas only
within this block.) Vanilla ER has both the deepest spike and the largest area
under group norm ($41.2$ pp, $34.9$), with batch norm milder on both ($11.8$ pp,
$17.5$); NCL is worse still ($55.1$ and $52.6$, the largest in the block),
mirroring its depth regression. The adaptive curriculum closes the gap an order of
magnitude faster, dropping the area to $3.6$ (batch norm) and $1.7$ (group norm):
the past task barely leaves its plateau through the switch. The NCL contrast is the cleanest separation in the study
between acting in schedule space (the curriculum) and acting in parameter space
(NCL): on this deeper backbone NCL's path-finding preconditioner makes the gap
\emph{larger} than vanilla ER ($25.8$ pp depth under batch norm, the opposite of
its rot-MNIST behaviour), while the curriculum's benefit is robust to depth and
normalisation.

\paragraph{Generalisation across corruptions and sequence length.} The
generalisation block (three seeds, group-norm ship config) pairs the curriculum
against a vanilla-ER control on shifts the method was not tuned on
(Table~\ref{tab:res_cifar_gen}). On the two-task novel corruptions the curriculum
generalises cleanly: depth falls from $46.3$ to $7.3$ pp on shot noise and from
$8.4$ to $3.9$ pp on contrast. The three-task sequence is the exception --- there
the curriculum underperforms its vanilla control ($32.8$ vs $4.4$ pp, with a
large seed variance of $\pm 14.5$ pp), an anomaly that does not appear in the
batch-norm variant of the same sequence (Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}). We
read this as the adaptive schedule interacting poorly with several closely spaced
group-norm transitions rather than a single one, and flag it as a boundary of the
method's reliability rather than evidence against the two-task result; final
accuracy stays comparable to vanilla throughout.

\subsection{Long-Sequence rot-MNIST}
\label{subsec:res_longseq}

The two-task design isolates a single transition. To check that the curriculum
holds across several boundaries, we ran a five-task rot-MNIST sequence
(rotations $[0, 30, 60, 90, 120]^\circ$, four transitions) at momentum $0.9$
(Table~\ref{tab:res_longseq}).

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{%
        \small
        \begin{tabular}{l cc}
            \hline

The adaptive curriculum carries past a single transition: it roughly halves the
vanilla-ER gap ($8.9 \rightarrow 4.4$ pp) across four boundaries while slightly
\emph{improving} final accuracy ($0.889 \rightarrow 0.896$). NCL reaches the
lowest depth of the four ($2.5$ pp) --- consistent with
\citet{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess} reporting its advantage at higher
task counts --- but at a large accuracy cost ($0.815$, $8$ pp below the
curriculum). The $\lambda_{\min}$ floor makes little difference to depth here.
As on the two-task MLP, the per-boundary bends are small for every condition
(\texttt{gap\_area} $\le 1.5$; Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}) --- rot-MNIST
reconverges quickly after each switch --- so what accumulates across the sequence
is permanent forgetting, not a hanging transient. Figure~\ref{fig:longseq} shows
this directly: vanilla ER (brown) steps down at each of the four boundaries and
holds the lowest plateau, while the adaptive curricula keep a higher plateau
throughout; NCL stays flattest but lowest, the accuracy cost again. The
curriculum thus keeps the property that matters across multiple transitions: a
low gap at no accuracy cost.

\subsection{Summary of Findings}
\label{subsec:res_summary}

Across blocks, four results stand out. (i) On rot-MNIST the stability gap is
dominated by the first-step magnitude asymmetry ($71\%$), with a small trajectory
residual ($10\%$) that survives exact gradients and balancing --- the residual
the curriculum targets. (ii) The adaptive $\lambda$-curriculum with momentum
reduces gap depth from $15.9$ to $3.7$ pp at matched accuracy, where momentum
alone does nothing and the curriculum alone leaves a stochastic residual: the
two are complementary, as predicted. (iii) The result carries to a ResNet-18 on
CIFAR-10 under both normalisations and to novel two-task corruptions, where the
curriculum beats both vanilla ER and NCL on depth and worst-case retention at
matched accuracy. (iv) The known boundaries are an accuracy drift for the linear
curriculum at momentum $0.0$, and a high-variance failure of the adaptive
schedule on a three-task group-norm sequence. Underlying all four is a
distinction the depth headline can hide: how deep the gap goes and how much
accuracy it costs before recovering are separate axes. The decomposition shows
the second one directly --- once the magnitude spike is removed, the trajectory
residual remains as a gradual \emph{bend} (G4, depth $1.9$ pp but a visible area),
the hanging curve the theory predicts. On the shallow MLP that transient closes in
a few steps and costs little; on the deeper CIFAR ResNet it stays open for
hundreds of steps and dominates the gap, and the curriculum's largest wins are
there. Throughout, the area is referenced against the recovered level so that
permanent forgetting --- NCL's apparent instability --- is not mistaken for a
transient. The discussion
interprets these against the theory of Sections~\ref{sec:motivation}
and~\ref{sec:theory}.
