\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:research_questions}

The two-level account and tracking bound of Section~\ref{sec:motivation} make falsifiable predictions about the gap and each gate. The experiments are organised around four questions. The first three are answered on rotated MNIST with a small MLP, where the gap can be probed step by step across many conditions and seeds; the fourth asks whether those answers survive a deeper backbone and a stronger task shift.

\paragraph{RQ1: The account.} Does the gap separate into a deterministic arc of the intended trajectory and overshoot of the followed path? Concretely: do the step-level interventions (shortening the first steps, removing gradient noise) act on the drop and leave the arc; does remixing the boundary gradients at matched step length, which alters the Level-One field rather than any Level-Two driver, fail to remove the drop; does placing the optimiser on the reference path remove the arc and leave the overshoot; and does only fixing path and step together close the gap, while the permanent stability--plasticity cost stays visible as reduced plasticity?

\paragraph{RQ2: The scalar feedback gate.} Does the $\lambda$-curriculum reduce the gap by timing the new-task push, and how do its ingredients divide the work? Four sub-questions probe the design:
\begin{description}
    \item[RQ2a (linear ramp).] Does gap depth fall as the ramp length $N$ grows, consistent with the $O(1/N)$ bound? And does the one-sided schedule beat a symmetric learning-rate warm-up at matched $N$, or is ``small steps early'' the whole effect?
    \item[RQ2b (adaptive schedule).] Does the gradient-norm feedback signal match the best fixed ramp while removing the knob $N$?
    \item[RQ2c ($\lambda_{\min}$ floor).] Does a small floor $\lambda_{\min} > 0$ recover early plasticity at a modest depth cost?
    \item[RQ2d (momentum).] Does momentum compose with the curriculum, closing the stochastic residual and restoring final accuracy, while leaving the depth of vanilla ER unchanged?
\end{description}

\paragraph{RQ3: The directional feedforward gate.} Does asymmetric PER reduce the gap as its filter strengthens, at what plasticity cost, and where does a feedforward gate fail? The account predicts one failure axis, momentum, whose accumulated velocity rebuilds the push the filter shrinks one step at a time.

\paragraph{RQ4: Generalisation.} Do the findings carry to a deeper backbone (ResNet-18) and a stronger task shift (CIFAR-10 domain corruption): does each gate still cut the gap at matched final accuracy, and do the momentum signatures of RQ2d and RQ3 reproduce there in a momentum cross?
