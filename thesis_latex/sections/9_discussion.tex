blurry boundary argument note: The two differ in what they hold fixed, and this is what makes the curriculum the cleaner experiment for the stability gap. A blurry stream changes the data distribution and the loss composition together: as $T_1$ samples enter the batch the gradient reweights, but the very set of examples the model trains on also changes, so any reduction in forgetting cannot be attributed to the gradual reweighting alone. A blurry stream also carries no lasting retention pressure: once the transition completes and the batch is entirely $T_1$, the old-task data leaves the stream, so nothing continues to anchor the model to the old optimum and forgetting can resume unchecked. The $\lambda$-curriculum instead leaves data sampling and buffer contents untouched and varies a single scalar, the weight on the current-task loss, and it retains an explicit replay term even after the new task has fully ramped in. It therefore isolates the gradual reweighting as the one ingredient responsible for any change in the gap, and sustains the retention signal that a blurry stream loses. In this sense it suports the blurry-boundary finding that softer transitions forget less, while separating the causal variables that line of work leaves entangled.
