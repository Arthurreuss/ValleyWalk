\section{Motivation: The Gap as a Symptom of a Discontinuous Loss}
\label{sec:motivation}

This section develops the argument that the stability gap is, in its dominant components, the downstream symptom of one structural feature of online continual learning: the loss the optimiser follows changes discontinuously at each task boundary. Section~\ref{subsec:homotopy} states the homotopy that the $\lambda$-curriculum implements. Section~\ref{subsec:decomposition} decomposes the gap into three additive contributors and identifies which the curriculum addresses. Section~\ref{subsec:pathfinding_vs_shaping} contrasts path-finding with landscape-shaping, and Section~\ref{subsec:envelope} states the envelope-theorem bound on the trajectory contributor.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The Homotopy Family $L_\lambda$}{The Homotopy Family L\_lambda}}
\label{subsec:homotopy}

Standard online continual learning trains on a sequence of objectives that change discontinuously. During $T_0$ the optimiser descends $L_{\text{replay}} = L_{T_0}$ alone. The moment $T_1$ begins, the objective switches in a single step to $L_{\text{replay}} + L_{\text{current}} = L_{T_0} + L_{T_1}$. The parameter $\theta$ does not move during the switch, but the surface the optimiser follows --- and the location of its minimum --- jumps from $\theta_0^*$ to $\theta_{\text{joint}}^*$. The $\lambda$-curriculum replaces this jump with a one-parameter family of losses,
\begin{equation}
    L_\lambda(\theta) = L_{\text{replay}}(\theta) + \lambda\, L_{\text{current}}(\theta),
    \qquad \lambda \in [0, 1],
    \label{eq:homotopy}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda = 0$ recovers pure replay (optimum at $\theta_0^* := \arg\min L_{\text{replay}}$) and $\lambda = 1$ recovers the joint loss (optimum at $\theta_{\text{joint}}^*$). Ramping $\lambda$ along a continuous schedule defines two trajectories: the \emph{optimum path} $\Theta^*(\lambda) := \arg\min_\theta L_\lambda(\theta)$, a continuously differentiable curve from $\theta_0^*$ to $\theta_{\text{joint}}^*$ under standard regularity assumptions, and the \emph{SGD trajectory} $\theta(t)$, the actual sequence of iterates. The fourth panel of Figure~\ref{fig:loss_landscape_visualization} shows the reshaped basin for a small $\lambda$.

\subsection{Three-Contributor Decomposition}
\label{subsec:decomposition}

Consider the first vanilla-ER step at the start of $T_1$. Because $\theta_0^*$ is a stationary point of $L_{\text{replay}}$, the true replay gradient there is near zero, and the update is
\begin{equation}
    \theta_1 \approx \theta_0^* - \eta\left(\varepsilon(\theta_0^*) + g_{\text{new}}(\theta_0^*)\right),
    \label{eq:first_step}
\end{equation}
where $g_{\text{new}} := \nabla L_{\text{current}}$ and $\varepsilon$ is the zero-mean finite-buffer estimator error. This step has three features, each driving a distinct contribution to the subsequent dip on $T_0$.

\paragraph{Magnitude asymmetry ($G_{\text{mag}}$).} The new-task gradient $g_{\text{new}}(\theta_0^*)$ is generically $O(1)$, while $\varepsilon$ is mean-zero and $O(1/\sqrt{k})$ in the buffer size $k$. The first step is therefore dominated by $-\eta\, g_{\text{new}}(\theta_0^*)$, and a second-order expansion of the replay loss gives
\begin{equation}
    L_{\text{replay}}(\theta_1) - L_{\text{replay}}(\theta_0^*) \approx
    \tfrac{1}{2}\eta^2\, g_{\text{new}}^\top H_{\text{replay}}(\theta_0^*)\, g_{\text{new}} > 0,
    \label{eq:gmag}
\end{equation}
whenever $H_{\text{replay}}$ has positive curvature along $g_{\text{new}}$. This is the dominant contributor and a direct consequence of the discontinuity.

\paragraph{Estimator bias ($G_{\text{est}}$).} On any individual run $\varepsilon \neq 0$ and adds a stochastic kick. Following \citet{aljundi2019gradientbasedsampleselection}, a finite buffer is a finite-sample estimator of the past data, and the per-step error contributes a depth that does not vanish under any schedule because it is independent of the loss composition. This contributor is reported but is not the one the curriculum is designed to fix.

\paragraph{Trajectory effect ($G_{\text{traj}}$).} Even with the exact joint-loss gradient and no estimator error, the geometric observation of \citet{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess} (Section~\ref{subsec:kao}) remains: an instantaneous switch from $\lambda = 0$ to $\lambda = 1$ lands the iterate off $\Theta^*(1)$, where the replay loss is strictly higher than at the joint optimum. This is the contributor the envelope-theorem argument addresses.

The contributors act at different characteristic steps --- $G_{\text{mag}}$ at step one, $G_{\text{est}}$ across all steps, $G_{\text{traj}}$ over the first equilibration window --- which lets an ablation isolate each empirically (Section~\ref{sec:methods}).

\subsection{Path-Finding versus Landscape-Shaping}
\label{subsec:pathfinding_vs_shaping}

Methods can be organised by what they do with the discontinuity rather than by what curvature information they use. \emph{Path-finding} accepts the discontinuity: the objective still jumps at the boundary, and the intervention reshapes \emph{how} the optimiser moves, replacing plain SGD with a preconditioned step $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta\, M(\theta_t)\,\hat{g}_t$. Here $M$ is a second-order quantity --- the inverse Fisher of EWC \cite{Kirkpatrick_2017}, the precision matrix of NCL \cite{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess}, or the feasible-set projection of GEM \cite{lopezpaz2022gradientepisodicmemorycontinual}. Gradient balancing, the ``balanced'' ER mode, is path-finding too: it leaves the schedule discontinuous but rescales the gradient so the first step is not dominated by $g_{\text{new}}$.

\emph{Landscape-shaping} removes the discontinuity. It modifies the objective in a neighbourhood of the boundary so the surface the optimiser descends is continuous through the transition, leaving the geometry of the step to SGD's natural step-size adaptation. The $\lambda$-curriculum is the canonical example: $\hat{g}_t = \nabla\!\left(L_{\text{replay}} + \lambda(t)\, L_{\text{current}}\right)(\theta_t)$ with $\lambda(t)$ a continuous schedule from $0$ to $1$. Three differences follow. Preconditioners depend on the surface they are built over --- NCL's factors are computed on linear weights only --- whereas $\lambda(t)$ multiplies the loss and flows through the backward pass to every parameter. Any preconditioner that normalises the gradient breaks SGD's step-size adaptation, the source of the accuracy cost incurred by gradient balancing, while landscape-shaping leaves it intact. And the preconditioned step requires a per-step matrix--vector product, whereas the curriculum adds one scalar multiply per backward pass.

\subsection{The Envelope-Theorem Bound}
\label{subsec:envelope}

The reason a continuous schedule removes the trajectory contributor is that the replay loss cannot rise along the optimum path. At the optimum the first-order condition is $\nabla L_{\text{replay}}(\Theta^*(\lambda)) + \lambda\, \nabla L_{\text{current}}(\Theta^*(\lambda)) = 0$. Differentiating it implicitly in $\lambda$ and substituting into the chain rule for $\tfrac{d}{d\lambda} L_{\text{replay}}(\Theta^*(\lambda))$ yields
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{d\lambda} L_{\text{replay}}(\Theta^*(\lambda)) =
    \lambda\, \nabla L_{\text{current}}^\top H_\lambda^{-1}\, \nabla L_{\text{current}} \geq 0,
    \label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
since $\lambda \geq 0$ and $H_\lambda = H_{\text{replay}} + \lambda H_{\text{current}} \succ 0$ along the path. The replay loss is monotone non-decreasing along $\Theta^*(\lambda)$, so no overshoot is possible on the optimum path. An adiabatic-tracking argument shows the SGD iterates stay within $O(1/N)$ of the optimum path for ramp length $N$, so
\begin{equation}
    \sup_t L_{\text{replay}}(\theta(t)) \leq L_{\text{replay}}(\theta_{\text{joint}}^*) + O(1/N).
    \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
The first term is the unavoidable cost of joint training; the $O(1/N)$ term is the trajectory contributor that the curriculum eliminates as $N$ grows. This is the prediction tested in the experiments: under the linear curriculum, gap depth should fall as $N$ grows, while joint-task accuracy stays close to vanilla ER because the step-size adaptation is preserved. Two refinements act on errors the continuous-limit argument abstracts away. A $\lambda_{\min}$ floor, $\lambda(t) = \max(\lambda_{\min}, t/N)$, starts the homotopy at an interior point where the current-task gradient is already present, trading a slightly higher gap floor for faster early progress on $T_1$. Momentum averages the per-step gradient over recent steps, suppressing the stochastic oscillation that survives the curriculum without changing where the gradient points; it therefore cannot substitute for the curriculum, since the one-sided first step is deterministic.
