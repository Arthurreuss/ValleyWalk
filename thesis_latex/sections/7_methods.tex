\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

This section describes the empirical testbed (Section~\ref{subsec:testbed}), the metrics that summarise each run (Section~\ref{subsec:metrics}), and the full set of conditions (Sections~\ref{subsec:overview}--\ref{subsec:cifar}) that test the predictions of Sections~\ref{sec:motivation} and~\ref{sec:theory}. Each experimental block maps to one of the research questions of Section~\ref{sec:proposed_approach} and to a falsifiable prediction derived earlier.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\subsubsection{Testbed}
\label{subsec:testbed}

The experiments run on two testbeds of deliberately different scale. A small, fast testbed --- rotated MNIST on a two-layer MLP --- carries the dense mechanistic sweep, where the gap is probed step by step across many conditions and seeds. A larger testbed --- a domain-shift CIFAR-10 on a CIFAR-adapted ResNet-18 --- tests whether the findings survive a deeper backbone and a stronger task shift, the regime where the smoothness assumption behind the bound of Section~\ref{sec:theory} weakens. Both testbeds share one design choice: the label space and task structure are held fixed and only the input distribution shifts, and the headline sequences use the minimal two tasks, so there is exactly one transition and exactly one instance of the stability gap, with no interference between overlapping gaps.

\paragraph{Datasets.}

The small benchmark is rotated MNIST (rot-MNIST). Following \citet{delange2023continualevaluationlifelonglearning}, rotations are measured from a $-90^\circ$ baseline: the first task $T_0$ presents the digits at $-90^\circ$, and the second task $T_1$ applies a further $90^\circ$ rotation to bring them to $0^\circ$. Each task uses the standard $60{,}000$ training and $10{,}000$ test images. The $90^\circ$ rotation makes the two input distributions well separated while keeping the tasks structurally identical, with the same label space and the same underlying digit identities. A five-task variant (rotations $[-90,-60,-30,0,30]^\circ$) is used for the long-sequence block of Section~\ref{subsec:longseq}.

The large benchmark is a domain-shift CIFAR-10: $T_0$ uses clean CIFAR-10 and $T_1$ uses the Gaussian-noise corruption at severity $3$. The ten classes are shared, so as in rot-MNIST only the input distribution shifts and two tasks produce a single transition. A three-task variant ($[\text{none}, \text{Gaussian noise}, \text{shot noise}]$) and two alternative corruptions are used in the generalisation block of Section~\ref{subsec:cifar}.

\subsubsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{subsubsec:eval_protocol}

Throughout training, every task's test accuracy is evaluated every $10$ steps. At each task transition the cadence increases to every step, so the dip is recorded at full resolution where it occurs. The two benchmarks differ only in how long this per-step cadence is held. On rot-MNIST it runs for the entire new task: one online epoch is short (${\approx}234$ steps), so the whole post-transition trajectory is captured per step. On CIFAR-10, where a task spans ten epochs, the per-step cadence is held for the first $50$ steps after the transition, enough to cover the gap, and then drops back to every $10$ steps for the rest of the task.

A stability-gap tracker snapshots the pre-task accuracy on each past task before the transition and records the resulting (step, accuracy) series, from which it computes the scalar summaries of Section~\ref{subsubsec:gap_metrics}. A gradient tracker records, at the same per-step cadence, the directional and magnitude fidelity of the replay-buffer gradient against the deterministic empirical past-task gradient $g_{\text{true}}$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:grad_metrics}); it is enabled for the decomposition conditions only.

\subsubsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

All metrics are recorded automatically and aggregated later. Unless noted otherwise, each is reported as the seed mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over a condition's seeds (five for the rot-MNIST and CIFAR headline blocks, three for the CIFAR generalisation block); \texttt{gap\_area} is reported as a seed mean only, and \texttt{recovery\_steps} only when a recovery occurs within the record. This section explains the most important ones; the complete definitions of every logged metric are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:metric_defs}.

\paragraph{Stability-gap metrics.}
\label{subsubsec:gap_metrics}

These are computed from the dense per-step series. Writing $a_j^{\text{pre}}$ for the pre-task accuracy on past task $j$ and $a_j(t)$ for its accuracy at step $t$ of the new task, the headline measure is
\begin{equation*}
    \texttt{gap\_depth} = \max_j \left( a_j^{\text{pre}} - \min_t a_j(t) \right),
\end{equation*}
the worst single point of instability across the record window. It is the primary outcome in every contrast: each prediction of Sections~\ref{sec:motivation} and~\ref{sec:theory} is stated as a claim about how \texttt{gap\_depth} moves between conditions. A companion scalar, \texttt{gap\_area}, integrates the past-task accuracy shortfall --- the region where accuracy sits below its pre-task baseline --- over the full new-task record, folding depth and duration into one number; \texttt{recovery\_steps} reports how many steps after the transition every past task first returns to within $90\%$ of its pre-task baseline, and is undefined when no sub-threshold dip occurs. Both are reported alongside depth but are secondary. Their precise definitions, and the fixed-window \texttt{max\_drop} kept only for back-comparability, are in Table~\ref{tab:gap_metrics}.

\paragraph{Continual-learning metrics.}

To place the gap in the standard continual-learning frame, we also report the continual-evaluation metrics of \citet{delange2023continualevaluationlifelonglearning}, computed from the task-boundary accuracy matrix $R[i,j] = A(E_j, f_{T_i})$ that is logged for every run (so any downstream metric can be reconstructed). The two that carry the argument are average final accuracy (ACC), which checks that a curriculum buys lower depth without an accuracy cost, and the worst-case measures min-ACC and WC-ACC, which report retention at its lowest point rather than only at the end. FORG and the windowed worst-case forgetting/plasticity measures $\mathrm{WF}_w$/$\mathrm{WP}_w$ are reported for completeness; all definitions are in Table~\ref{tab:cl_metrics}.

\paragraph{Gradient-fidelity diagnostics.}
\label{subsubsec:grad_metrics}

For the decomposition block the gradient tracker compares the replay-buffer gradient $g_{\text{replay}}$ against the deterministic empirical past-task gradient $g_{\text{true}}$ (a separate full-data forward/backward pass, no sub-sampling). Two diagnostics measure how faithfully a finite buffer approximates the past data \citep{aljundi2019gradientbasedsampleselection}: the directional fidelity $\cos(g_{\text{replay}}, g_{\text{true}})$ and the magnitude fidelity $\|g_{\text{replay}}\|/\|g_{\text{true}}\|$. These supply the direct per-step evidence for the estimator-bias contributor of Section~\ref{subsec:decomp}. A third, $\|g_{\text{new}}\|/\|g_{\text{replay}}\|$, is the update-side magnitude asymmetry; its inverse $\|g_{\text{replay}}\|/\|g_{\text{new}}\|$ is the signal the adaptive curriculum of Section~\ref{subsec:curriculum} uses to set $\lambda(t)$. Logged names and exact definitions are in Table~\ref{tab:grad_metrics}.

Each diagnostic step adds one forward and backward pass over every past-task training sample ($60{,}000$ for rot-MNIST $T_0$), which is affordable on the MLP testbed but would dominate runtime on larger backbones, so the toggle stays off for the curriculum and CIFAR blocks, whose claims rest on \texttt{gap\_depth} and ACC rather than per-step gradient geometry.

\paragraph{Statistical comparison.}

Across conditions we compare metrics with the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the seed-paired difference. Seeds are paired by index across conditions, so the per-seed deltas $\Delta_s = m(C_a, s) - m(C_b, s)$ factor out the seed-induced variance that would otherwise dominate the five-seed sample. We prefer Wilcoxon over the paired $t$-test because \texttt{gap\_depth} and accuracy are bounded in $[0,1]$ and typically right-skewed near zero, so the $t$-test's normality assumption is suspect; the Wilcoxon test exchanges that assumption for the milder one that $\Delta_s$ is distributionally symmetric under the null. For equivalence-style claims, such as ``the adaptive curriculum matches the best linear curriculum'', we report the seed-paired difference with its bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval rather than a null-hypothesis test.

\subsubsection{Three-Contributor Decomposition}
\label{subsec:decomp}

\paragraph{Aim.} Isolate the three contributors of Section~\ref{subsec:decomposition} --- magnitude asymmetry ($G_{\text{mag}}$), estimator variance ($G_{\text{est}}$), and the trajectory effect ($G_{\text{traj}}$) --- and quantify each one's share of the total gap. This is the diagnostic question of RQ1.

\paragraph{Design.} Two of the three contributors are predicted to be downstream symptoms of the discontinuity at the task boundary; the third, estimator variance, is a property of finite replay buffers and persists at any schedule. Removing each contributor by construction and observing the residual gap depth identifies which contributors drive the dip. The path G1 $\rightarrow$ G2 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4 removes one source at a time (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}).

\begin{table*}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{p{2.8cm} p{2.4cm} p{2.6cm} p{3.0cm} p{3.0cm}}
        \hline
        Condition                   & Replay buffer    & Gradient handling               & Removed by construction          & Residual gap                                                          \\
        \hline
        G1 --- Vanilla ER           & $1$k reservoir   & unbalanced                      & nothing                          & magnitude + estimator + trajectory                                    \\
        G2 --- Full-data ER         & $60$k (all past) & unbalanced                      & estimator noise (exact gradient) & magnitude + trajectory                                                \\
        G3 --- Balanced ER          & $1$k reservoir   & unit-norm balanced              & magnitude asymmetry              & estimator + trajectory ({+}\,balancing cost)                          \\
        G4 --- Full-data + balanced & $60$k            & unit-norm balanced              & magnitude and estimator          & trajectory                                                            \\
        NCL (reference)             & none             & K-FAC precision preconditioning & ---                              & path-finding reference \citep{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess} \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}

    \label{tab:decomp}
\end{table*}

\paragraph{Gradient balancing.} The balanced conditions G3 and G4 compute the replay and new-task gradients in two separate backward passes, normalise each to unit norm, and combine them $50/50$, so the two terms enter the update with equal magnitude regardless of their raw sizes. This targets the first-step asymmetry of Section~\ref{subsec:decomposition}: at $\theta_0^*$ the replay gradient is near zero, so an unbalanced step is driven almost entirely by $g_{\text{new}}$, whereas after normalisation the replay direction carries equal weight from the first step. Balancing removes the magnitude asymmetry by construction, so $G_{\text{mag}}$ is exactly the gap-depth difference between an unbalanced step (G1) and a balanced one (G3).

\paragraph{Full-data conditions.} The full-data conditions G2 and G4 hold the entire past-task training set in the buffer and, at every step, compute the replay gradient on every stored sample exactly once. The resulting replay gradient is the deterministic empirical past-task gradient at the current parameters --- no sampling, no bootstrap variance. Vanilla ER (G1) draws $256$ samples with replacement, so its replay gradient is a stochastic estimator of this same quantity. The estimator contributor $G_{\text{est}}$ is therefore exactly the gap-depth difference between an estimator-driven step and an exact-gradient step, and $G_{\text{traj}}$ in G4 is the residual once both the estimator and the magnitude asymmetry are removed.

\paragraph{Decomposition shares.} Differences in \texttt{gap\_depth} along the path identify each contributor. The magnitude share ($G_{\text{mag}}$) is the drop from G1 to G3, removed by balancing alone; the estimator share ($G_{\text{est}}$) is the further drop from G3 to G4, the additional reduction from full data on top of balancing; and the trajectory share ($G_{\text{traj}}$) is the residual gap at G4 once both are removed, the trajectory contribution of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The G2 condition is a sanity check: $\texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G1}) - \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G2})$ should recover $G_{\text{est}}$ along an orthogonal path through the design.

\paragraph{NCL reference.} NCL is included as a path-finding baseline (Section~\ref{subsec:pathfinding_vs_shaping}): it accepts the discontinuity but preconditions each SGD step by the past-task precision matrix.

\paragraph{Prediction.} Gap depth reduces along the path G1 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4. The trajectory residual at G4 is small but non-zero (the prediction of \citet{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess}, Section~\ref{subsec:stability_gap}), and is the contributor the $\lambda$-curriculum should additionally suppress.

\subsubsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda}-Curriculum Sweep}
\label{subsec:curriculum}

\paragraph{Aim.} Test the envelope-theorem prediction of Section~\ref{sec:theory} --- that a continuous $\lambda$-schedule eliminates the trajectory contribution to the gap, with a transient that shrinks as $O(1/N)$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}) --- the $\lambda_{\min}$ trade-off, and the momentum cross-cut of RQ2d. Answers RQ2a--RQ2d.

\paragraph{Design.} All seven curriculum conditions (labelled C1--C7) are applied on top of standard ER ($1$k reservoir, no balancing, no NCL) so the curriculum's effect is isolated (Table~\ref{tab:curriculum}). As stated in Section~\ref{subsec:overview}, each is run at both momentum $0.0$ and $0.9$.

The adaptive schedule (C4) is self-paced. At $\theta_0^*$ the replay gradient is near zero by stationarity, so the ratio $r := \|g_{\text{replay}}\| / \|g_{\text{new}}\|$ is near zero and $\lambda$ starts near $0$. As the iterate leaves $\theta_0^*$, $\|g_{\text{replay}}\|$ grows while $\|g_{\text{new}}\|$ shrinks as the model fits $T_1$, so $r$ climbs and $\lambda \to 1$ with no explicit ramp length; the schedule ignores $N$ entirely. The floor $\lambda_{\min}$ (C5--C7) matters mainly for this adaptive variant: it stops the schedule from sitting at $\lambda \approx 0$ in the first steps, when the EMA estimate of $r$ is still close to zero.

\paragraph{Prediction.} \texttt{gap\_depth} should fall monotonically as $N$ grows from $50$ to $200$ (the $O(1/N)$ bound of Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}), while ACC stays close to vanilla ER, because the curriculum preserves SGD's natural step-size adaptation rather than rescaling the step the way gradient balancing does (RQ2a). The adaptive schedule should reach a comparable depth reduction without fixing $N$ (RQ2b). A small floor $\lambda_{\min} \in [0.05, 0.15]$ should restore early $T_1$ velocity --- the adaptive schedule otherwise sits near $\lambda \approx 0$ in the first steps and barely trains the new task --- giving faster early learning and higher final accuracy, at the cost of only a slight increase in gap depth (well short of vanilla-ER levels), for a better (depth, ACC) Pareto position than $\lambda_{\min} = 0$ (RQ2c).

The contrast between balanced ER (G3) and linear $N{=}200$ (C3) isolates the difference between acting on the magnitude symptom alone and acting on the schedule discontinuity: both should suppress depth, but only the curriculum should preserve ACC.

\paragraph{Momentum (RQ2d).}
\label{subsec:momentum}
Because every condition above (and the decomposition G-series) is run at both momentum $0.0$ and $0.9$, the same sweep tests the two momentum predictions of RQ2d. On top of the curriculum, momentum should reduce the seed-to-seed standard deviation in \texttt{gap\_depth} and the per-step oscillation amplitude on the $T_0$ accuracy curve, without changing the depth mean by much --- the residual it cleans up is the stochastic oscillation around the optimum path. Without the curriculum, momentum should \emph{not} reduce \texttt{gap\_depth}: depth for vanilla ER (G1) at momentum $0.9$ should match depth at momentum $0.0$, because the one-sided first step at the boundary is deterministic and its direction is unchanged. The contrast $\text{C3}$ vs.\ $\text{C3}_M$ measures the residual momentum removes; the contrast $\text{G1}$ vs.\ $\text{G1}_M$ tests the falsification, and a null there is the falsifiable prediction.

\subsubsection{Long-Sequence rot-MNIST}
\label{subsec:longseq}

\paragraph{Aim.} The two-task design isolates a single transition, which keeps the decomposition clean but leaves two questions open: whether the curriculum still suppresses the gap across several consecutive boundaries, and whether NCL recovers the advantage that \citet{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess} report at the higher task counts where path-finding methods are strongest.

\paragraph{Design.} A five-task rot-MNIST sequence with rotations $[-90, -60, -30, 0, 30]^\circ$, giving four consecutive transitions over a uniform $30^\circ$ step. The backbone, online regime, and dense per-step evaluation are those of the two-task block (Section~\ref{subsec:testbed}). Momentum is fixed at $0.9$: the C-series and the G1/G1$_M$ contrast already characterise the momentum trade at a single transition, so running both legs here would add little. Four conditions (Table~\ref{tab:longseq}) --- vanilla ER, tuned NCL, and the adaptive curriculum at $\lambda_{\min} \in \{0, 0.10\}$ --- each at five seeds. Gap-depth and accuracy are averaged over the four transitions.

\subsubsection{Generalisation: CIFAR-10}
\label{subsec:cifar}

\paragraph{Aim.} Confirm the direction of the rot-MNIST findings under a stronger task shift and a deeper, harder-to-analyse architecture --- the regime in which the assumptions behind the envelope-theorem bound weaken (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). The headline dataset is the two-task Gaussian-noise domain shift of Section~\ref{subsec:testbed}.

\paragraph{Training protocol.} The CIFAR-adapted ResNet-18 of Table~\ref{tab:resnet}, ten epochs per task --- at this depth ResNet-18 needs more than one pass to converge before the transition --- batch size $256$, SGD with learning rate $0.1$, and a replay buffer of $5{,}000$ samples. Momentum is fixed at $0.9$ (the better-performing leg from rot-MNIST); the two-leg momentum sweep of Section~\ref{subsec:overview} is not repeated here. Evaluation follows the scheme of Section~\ref{subsubsec:eval_protocol}: per-step for the first $50$ steps after the transition, every $10$ steps otherwise.

\paragraph{Headline conditions.} The headline block is six conditions: three methods --- vanilla ER, NCL, and the adaptive curriculum at $\lambda_{\min}=0.20$ --- each run under both BatchNorm and GroupNorm (Table~\ref{tab:cifar}). A linear curriculum is not run on CIFAR; $\lambda_{\min}=0.20$ is the single carried-over curriculum setting, the adaptive variant that gave the best final accuracy in the rot-MNIST $\lambda_{\min}$ sweep. NCL hyperparameters are pinned to the values that won the rot-MNIST tuning sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}). The two normalisations are crossed in deliberately: they govern the post-switch recovery dynamics. BatchNorm running statistics are disrupted by the input shift and re-adapt over the first steps of the new task, whereas GroupNorm carries no cross-batch statistics, so the pair separates the normalisation-driven part of the dip from the optimisation-driven part the curriculum targets.

\paragraph{Generalisation block.} A second CIFAR block tests whether the headline finding depends on the particular corruption or on having only a single transition. It pairs the shipped curriculum (adaptive $\lambda_{\min}=0.20$, buffer $5{,}000$) against a vanilla-ER control on three further settings: a two-task shot-noise shift, a two-task contrast shift, and a three-task sequence $[\text{none}, \text{Gaussian noise}, \text{shot noise}]$ (Table~\ref{tab:cifar_gen}). Each shift is run at both normalisations over three seeds. The paired vanilla control on every shift keeps the comparison interpretable: the question is whether the curriculum still beats vanilla on data it was not tuned on.

\paragraph{Prediction.} Qualitative carry-over of the rot-MNIST result: the adaptive curriculum should reduce gap depth relative to vanilla ER and at least match NCL on depth while preserving ACC, under both normalisations, and should keep beating its vanilla control on the novel corruptions of the generalisation block. The quantitative $O(1/N)$ scaling of the bound is not expected to hold tightly at this depth; deviations are reported and discussed alongside the results.
