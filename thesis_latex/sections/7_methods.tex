\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

This section describes the empirical testbeds (Section~\ref{subsec:setup}), the metrics that summarise each run (Section~\ref{subsec:metrics}), and the experimental blocks (Section~\ref{subsec:overview}) that answer the research questions of Section~\ref{sec:research_questions}. All code, experiment configurations, and analysis scripts are available at \url{https://github.com/Arthurreuss/ValleyWalk}.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\subsubsection{Testbeds}
\label{subsec:testbed}

Two testbeds of different scale carry the study. A small, fast one (rotated MNIST on a two-layer MLP) runs the dense mechanistic sweeps: the step-size ladder, the curriculum sweep, the asymmetric-PER damping sweep, and the momentum cross. A larger one (domain-shift CIFAR-10 on a CIFAR-adapted ResNet-18) tests whether the findings survive a deeper backbone and a stronger task shift, the regime where the local condition behind the bound of Section~\ref{subsec:reference} weakens. Both hold the label space and task structure fixed and shift only the input distribution, and both use two tasks in their main blocks, so there is exactly one transition and one instance of the stability gap; a five-task rot-MNIST sequence and a three-task CIFAR sequence serve as multi-boundary checks.

On rot-MNIST, $T_0$ presents the digits upright and $T_1$ rotates them by $90^\circ$, each task using the standard $60{,}000$ training and $10{,}000$ test images; the $90^\circ$ domain shift follows \citet{delange2023continualevaluationlifelonglearning}, which orients the pair as $-90^\circ \rightarrow 0^\circ$. On CIFAR-10, $T_0$ is clean and $T_1$ applies Gaussian-noise corruption at severity $3$ \citep{hendrycks2019robustness}; the ten classes are shared, so only the input distribution shifts. A three-task variant ($[\text{none}, \text{Gaussian}, \text{shot}]$) and two alternative corruptions appear in the generalisation block. The rot-MNIST MLP is cheap enough to sweep across many conditions and seeds and is where the local condition of Section~\ref{subsec:reference} is most plausible; the ResNet-18 is the deeper, more realistic counterpart. rot-MNIST runs online (one epoch per task); the ResNet needs several passes to converge before the transition, so the CIFAR block uses ten epochs per task. Optimiser, learning rate, and batch size are common across benchmarks; no method retunes the learning rate, so every comparison between methods runs at a matched step size (Section~\ref{subsec:gates}). The step size is held fixed in that role and manipulated in one block only, the ladder of Section~\ref{subsec:ladder}, which holds the objective, the buffer, and the update rule fixed and varies $\eta$ alone. Full backbone and protocol specifications are in Appendix~\ref{app:impl_models} (Tables~\ref{tab:mlp}--\ref{tab:training}).

Throughout training every task's test accuracy is evaluated every $10$ steps; at each transition the cadence rises to every step, so the dip is recorded at full resolution (the whole online epoch on rot-MNIST, a $250$-step window on CIFAR). A stability-gap tracker snapshots each past task's pre-switch accuracy and records the resulting (step, accuracy) series. Because the coarse pre-transition cadence leaves a short interval between the last pre-switch evaluation and the first post-update one, the per-step figures hold the last pre-switch level up to the boundary rather than interpolating across that interval; the past task is still training and at its plateau there, so the alternative would draw the drop as beginning before the switch. All reported metrics are computed from the logged samples themselves and are unaffected by this plotting convention. On the vanilla-ER reference runs only, a gradient tracker additionally records the fidelity of the replay-buffer gradient against the exact past-task gradient, at the same cadence; each such step costs a full pass over the past-task training set, which is why it is not run elsewhere, the ladder's long rungs least of all.

\subsubsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

Each metric is reported as the seed mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation; complete definitions are in Appendix~\ref{app:metric_defs}. The gap definition of Section~\ref{sec:motivation}, the transient excess below the level a task settles at, gives two primary metrics. With $a_j^{\text{pre}}$ the pre-switch baseline on past task $j$ and $a_j(t)$ its accuracy at step $t$, \texttt{gap\_depth} is the maximum instantaneous excursion $\max_j (a_j^{\text{pre}} - \min_t a_j(t))$, which Section~\ref{subsec:trajectory_problem} predicts to be overshoot-dominated. The second, \texttt{gap\_area\_end}, integrates the shortfall below the settled level: with reference $b_j = \min(a_j^{\text{pre}}, \bar{a}_j)$, where $\bar{a}_j$ is the accuracy the task eventually reaches, it sums $\max(0, b_j - a_j(t))$ over the new task's training steps and across past tasks. Anchoring to the recovered level implements the split of Section~\ref{subsec:reference}: a permanent step down to a lower plateau contributes to the standard forgetting metrics and near-zero to the area, while a true dip-and-recover registers in full. One consequence, revisited when reading the tables, is that two methods settling at different levels are measured against different lines, so a better-recovering method can carry the \emph{larger} area; a positive area marks transient disruption, not net cost, and we read it with final accuracy. The arc of Section~\ref{subsec:trajectory_problem} and the slow part of the noisy recovery live here; sharp noise-driven drops register in depth instead. A third metric, \texttt{recovery\_steps}, and the gradient-fidelity diagnostics are defined in Appendix~\ref{app:metric_defs}.

One clarification is needed for the ladder of Section~\ref{subsec:ladder}, the only block in which $\eta$ itself varies. \texttt{gap\_depth} is a maximum over accuracies and is therefore $\eta$-invariant by construction. \texttt{gap\_area\_end} is not: it is a trapezoidal integral of the drop curve against the step index, weighted by the spacing between records, with units accuracy${\cdot}$steps. Stretching a rung by $c = 0.1/\eta$ stretches its step axis by the same factor, so an identically shaped excursion reads $c$ times larger. Across the ladder the area is therefore taken in flow time, $A_\tau = \eta\,A_{\text{steps}}$, the integral of the same curve against $\tau = \sum_t \eta$; within a rung the two differ by a constant, and every block at fixed $\eta$ reports the step-axis value as before.

To place the gap in the standard frame we report the continual-evaluation metrics of \citet{delange2023continualevaluationlifelonglearning} from the task-boundary accuracy matrix: average final accuracy (ACC), which checks that a gate lowers the gap at preserved accuracy; worst-case retention (min-ACC, WC-ACC); and short-horizon plasticity ($\mathrm{WP}_{100}$). FORG and the windowed $\mathrm{WF}_w/\mathrm{WP}_w$ family are logged for completeness. Conditions are compared with the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on seed-paired differences, preferred over the $t$-test because five paired seeds cannot support a normality assumption on bounded metrics: the gap metrics floor at zero, where the strongest conditions cluster, and accuracy sits near its ceiling, so the seed differences are skewed rather than Gaussian. With five paired seeds the smallest two-sided $p$-value is $0.0625$, reached when all five differences share a sign; we read $p = 0.0625$ as complete directional agreement and let effect sizes separate large results from marginal ones.

\subsection{Experiments}
\label{subsec:overview}

Each block maps to one or more research questions and tests the corresponding predictions of Section~\ref{sec:motivation}. All conditions use five seeds and the protocol above unless noted. Vanilla ER at the standard operating point (D1: $1$k reservoir, $\eta = 0.1$, no schedule and no filter) is the common reference for every rot-MNIST block and, like the blocks it anchors, is run at both momentum settings.


\subsubsection{The Step-Size Ladder (RQ1)}
\label{subsec:ladder}

RQ1 asks for a limit, and the ladder takes it. A uniform rescaling of $\eta$ does not change the intended trajectory: forward Euler at step $\eta$ integrates the same flow $\dot\theta = -g(\theta)$ on a finer grid, so a smaller step follows the same path more accurately and reaches the same place having covered the same flow time $\tau = \sum_t \eta$. The ladder is therefore an isolation rather than one more comparison. Whatever shrinks as $\eta$ falls is discretisation, Level Two by definition; whatever survives belongs to the path the optimiser was trying to follow all along, which is the arc. The curriculum and every rebalancing of the two gradients deform the vector field itself and so cannot make this separation; the step size is the one knob that leaves the field alone.

Four constraints make the four rungs read as a limit rather than as four unrelated runs. \emph{Matched flow time}: the epoch count scales as $1/\eta$, so a rung at $\eta = 0.1/c$ runs $c$ epochs and every cell covers the same $\tau = 23.5$ over the new task. Without this a small-$\eta$ cell simply does not finish the transition, and its small gap is under-training rather than a result. \emph{A frozen $\theta_0^*$}: every cell warm-starts from one shared task-$0$ checkpoint (Appendix~\ref{app:impl_ladder}) rather than training $T_0$ at its own step size. Training $T_0$ at the cell's own $\eta$ would land it in a differently sharp minimum, since less gradient noise means less implicit flattening, and the replay loss accelerates along the trajectory as $\dot\theta^\top H_{\text{rep}}\, \dot\theta$, so a sharper past-task Hessian \emph{enlarges} the arc: the confound points in the direction of the hypothesis and has to go. With it gone, $\theta_0^*$ and the replay buffer are identical across the four rungs at a given buffer arm, momentum, and seed, and $\eta$ is the only difference between them. \emph{A matched record grid}: the dense evaluation cadence is also $c$, so every cell records the same number of samples at the same spacing in flow time. \emph{A cadence counted from the boundary}: inside the dense window the cadence fires on the step index within the task rather than on the global step, because $c$ need not divide the $235$-batch epoch and a global-step modulus would otherwise place the first post-switch sample at an arbitrary offset into the task, skipping exactly the steps the spike lives in.

Table~\ref{tab:ladder} gives the rungs: $\eta = 0.1$ at one epoch, the standard operating point of every other block; $\eta = 0.1/3 \approx 0.033$ at three; $\eta = 0.01$ at ten; and $\eta = 0.001$ at a hundred. The first three settle the $\mu = 0$ limit; the fourth is there for the momentum leg, which needs a further decade before it converges for the reason given below. Each runs on two buffer arms, the $1$k reservoir of the standard protocol and a $60$k buffer whose replay gradient is the exact empirical past-task gradient (Appendix~\ref{app:impl_er}), and at momentum $0.0$ and $0.9$. The exact arm switches the estimator variance off at source, so an excursion that survives both the small step and the exact gradient is left with no Level-Two driver to attribute it to.

The momentum leg carries a second reading. Momentum multiplies the asymptotic step by $1/(1-\mu)$, so the $\mu = 0.9$ rungs sit at effective steps $1.0$, $0.33$, $0.1$ and $0.01$ against the $\mu = 0$ rungs' $0.1$, $0.033$, $0.01$ and $0.001$. Two diagonals are therefore exact, $(\eta = 0.01, \mu = 0.9)$ against $(\eta = 0.1, \mu = 0)$ and $(\eta = 0.001, \mu = 0.9)$ against $(\eta = 0.01, \mu = 0)$, and they are what the reading rests on. The same arithmetic is why the momentum leg needs the fourth rung at all: its three coarsest cells span the effective range in which the $\mu = 0$ leg had not yet converged. A momentum cell that behaves like its matched $\mu = 0$ partner locates momentum's boundary signature in the effective step; one that does not locates it in the accumulator. Momentum is not a reparametrisation of the flow, however, so the $\mu = 0.9$ leg is flow-matched only within itself: comparisons are drawn within a momentum setting, and the matched-step diagonal is a secondary read.

Because forward Euler's global error is $O(\eta)$, a residual metric should behave as $A(\eta) = A_0 + k\eta$ over the small-$\eta$ rungs, and the limit is estimated by fitting that line rather than asserted from the smallest rung; the fit returns $A_0$ with an error bar, and a departure from linearity at $\eta = 0.1$ is itself the signature of the discrete instability the account holds responsible for the first-step spike. The two levels then predict opposite behaviour for the two metrics. Depth, which records the overshoot, should fall towards zero down the ladder, and fastest on the exact arm. The area in flow time, which carries the arc, should not: it should converge on a positive $A_0$ that no further reduction of the step size reaches.

\subsubsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda}-Curriculum Sweep (RQ2, RQ1)}
\label{subsec:curriculum}

The curriculum sweep tests the scalar feedback gate one ingredient at a time. The linear ramps C1--C3 test the $O(1/N)$ prediction of Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}. The ladder of Section~\ref{subsec:ladder} sets the bar they have to clear: shrinking every step suppresses the overshoot too, and does so with no method at all, but it buys the reduction with a run stretched by the same factor and with whatever accuracy the shortened steps fail to reach in the budget. The curriculum's claim is therefore not that it lowers the gap but that it lowers the gap at the standard step size, in the standard number of steps, and on one side of the update only. All conditions run on top of standard ER ($1$k reservoir, $\eta = 0.1$, the two gradients summed as they come), so the schedule is the only changed variable (Table~\ref{tab:curriculum}). The adaptive schedule (C4) is the feedback variant of Section~\ref{subsec:gates}, a clipped exponential moving average of the gradient-norm ratio; it starts near $0$ at $\theta_0^*$ by stationarity and self-terminates when the gradients rebalance, with no ramp length to choose (mechanics in Appendix~\ref{app:impl_er}). The floor $\lambda_{\min}$ (C5--C7) keeps the schedule off $\lambda \approx 0$ during the first steps and buys back early plasticity. These two are design details of the shipped configuration; the ingredient sweep that settles them is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:curr_full}.

C8 combines the practical configuration (C7) with the exact $60$k replay gradient of the ladder's exact arm, fixing the path \emph{and} switching the estimator variance off at source. Its role is a substitution argument. At momentum $0$ the curriculum leaves a residual transient; if that residual is buffer noise, the exact gradient should remove it, and what C7 is missing is then not a second repair of the path but a variance reducer. Only one is affordable at a $1$k memory. Momentum averages successive replay estimates inside the velocity buffer and so lowers the variance of the restoring gradient while storing no additional samples, which makes C7$_M$ the cheap substitute for C8's $60$k buffer rather than a fortunate pairing. The comparison also splits momentum's two roles, since a buffer that leaves no variance to reduce isolates whatever momentum still contributes as new-task acceleration. C8 breaks the limited-memory premise and is diagnostic only; C7 remains the practical configuration.

\subsubsection{Momentum Cross (RQ2, RQ3)}
\label{subsec:momentum}

The vanilla-ER reference, every cell of the step-size ladder S1--S3, every curriculum condition C1--C8, and the asymmetric-PER sweep legs P1--P2 at the settings noted in Table~\ref{tab:per} are run at momentum $0.0$ and $0.9$; momentum-on runs carry an $M$ subscript (e.g.\ $\text{S1}_M$). The cross tests the composability prediction of Section~\ref{subsec:gates}: whether momentum helps or hurts a method's gap should depend on where the method attenuates the push. A five-task rot-MNIST sequence (rotations $[0,30,60,90,120]^\circ$, four transitions, five seeds) repeats the three-way comparison of vanilla ER, the practical curriculum, and asymmetric PER at both momentum settings, as a multi-boundary check (Appendix~\ref{app:longseq_full}).

\subsubsection{Asymmetric-PER \texorpdfstring{$\delta$}{delta} Sweep (RQ3)}
\label{subsec:per_asym}

The damping sweep tests the directional feedforward gate: does the gap shrink as the filter strengthens, at what plasticity cost, and where does the gate fail? Asymmetric PER implements Equation~\eqref{eq:asym_per} \citep{amari1998natural}; along an eigendirection of replay curvature $\rho$ the gain is $\delta/(\rho+\delta)$, so $\delta \to \infty$ recovers plain ER and $\delta \to 0$ concentrates the push in replay-flat directions, with the restoring gradient $g_{\text{rep}}$ at full strength throughout (Section~\ref{subsec:gates}). The push is applied matrix-free by conjugate gradients over Fisher-vector products, and the Fisher is re-estimated at every step from the current parameters on a sampled replay batch (Appendix~\ref{app:impl_per}). When momentum is enabled, it acts downstream of the gate: the filtered direction enters the velocity accumulator, and the accumulated velocity is applied unfiltered; preconditioning the velocity instead is a distinct design, discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:disc_outlook}.

Table~\ref{tab:per} lays out the sweep: $\delta \in \{1.0, 0.3, 0.1, 0.03\}$ crossed with two buffer legs, each at both momentum settings. The \emph{standard} leg ($1$k reservoir) matches the curriculum's practical regime, while the \emph{full-buffer} leg makes the replay gradient exact, switching off the variance driver as on the ladder's exact arm, so the remaining area measures the deterministic arc and the sweep reads out directly whether the gate shrinks the arc itself. Two controls guard the reading: a solver control reruns the strongest filter at $60$ CG iterations to separate gate properties from an under-converged solve (Appendix~\ref{app:impl_per}), and a symmetric control preconditions the summed gradient by the same replay Fisher, isolating the effect of leaving the restoring gradient untouched (Appendix~\ref{app:per_sym}).

\subsubsection{Generalisation: CIFAR-10 (RQ4)}
\label{subsec:cifar}

The CIFAR-10 block tests whether both gates and the momentum signatures carry to a deeper backbone and a stronger task shift. All CIFAR conditions use the ResNet-18 protocol of Table~\ref{tab:training} (ten epochs per task, buffer $5{,}000$). The headline conditions run at momentum $0.9$ (Table~\ref{tab:cifar}, top), each method crossed with BatchNorm and GroupNorm because the two respond differently at the switch: the input shift disrupts BatchNorm's running statistics, which must re-adapt during the first steps, while GroupNorm uses no cross-batch statistics, so its transient reflects optimisation dynamics alone. The curriculum runs as the adaptive schedule at $\lambda_{\min}=0.20$; asymmetric PER runs at $\delta=0.1$, a strong rot-MNIST setting. Both carry their rot-MNIST configurations without a CIFAR-specific sweep, so they meet the deeper backbone on equal footing. A momentum cross (Table~\ref{tab:cifar}, bottom) adds momentum-$0$ runs of all three methods at GroupNorm, repeating the three-way composability test where the transient is free of normalisation-statistics effects; because momentum also drives convergence on this backbone, accuracy is compared within a momentum setting. A further generalisation block pairs the practical curriculum against a paired vanilla-ER control on shifts it was never tuned on (a two-task shot-noise shift, a two-task contrast shift, and a three-task $[\text{none}, \text{Gaussian}, \text{shot}]$ sequence, each at both normalisations over three seeds) to test whether the result depends on the particular corruption or on having a single transition.
