\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

This section describes the empirical testbed (Section~\ref{subsec:testbed}), the metrics that summarise each run (Section~\ref{subsec:metrics}), and the full set of conditions (Sections~\ref{subsec:overview}--\ref{subsec:cifar}) that test the predictions of Sections~\ref{sec:account} and~\ref{sec:theory}. Each experimental block maps to one of the research questions of Section~\ref{sec:proposed_approach} and to a falsifiable prediction derived earlier.

\subsection{Testbed}
\label{subsec:testbed}

\subsubsection{Dataset: Rotated MNIST}

The primary sweep uses rotated MNIST (rot-MNIST) as a two-task sequence: the first task $T_0$ presents the digits at $0^\circ$ and the second task $T_1$ presents the same digits rotated by $90^\circ$. Each task uses the standard $60{,}000$ training and $10{,}000$ test images.

The $90^\circ$ rotation makes the two tasks visually distinct --- their input distributions are well separated --- while keeping them structurally identical, with the same label space and the same underlying digit identities. This makes the gradient dynamics at the transition maximally informative: the tasks differ enough that the new-task gradient is large at $\theta_0^*$, yet are similar enough that the replay buffer remains directly representative of the past data distribution. Two tasks produce exactly one transition, and so exactly one instance of the stability gap, which keeps the analysis clean: there is no interference between several overlapping gaps.

\subsubsection{Model: MLP}

The backbone for the rot-MNIST sweep is a two-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) with ReLU activations (Table~\ref{tab:mlp}; full details in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}).

The MLP is chosen over a deeper network for the rot-MNIST sweep on deliberate grounds. It has a simpler loss landscape, with fewer spurious curvature effects unrelated to the curriculum dynamics; it trains in seconds, which matters because the sweep spans many conditions and seeds; and it stays close to the adiabatic regime in which the Hessian assumptions behind the envelope-theorem bound of Section~\ref{sec:theory} hold approximately, so that bound is sharply testable. A ResNet-18 backbone is used for the CIFAR-10 generalisation experiment of Section~\ref{subsec:cifar}; details are in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

\subsubsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{subsubsec:eval_protocol}

Dense per-step evaluation is used during the first $500$ steps of $T_1$ training. A stability-gap tracker snapshots the pre-task accuracy on the $T_0$ test set before $T_1$ begins, evaluates the model on the $T_0$ test set at every training step, and records the resulting (step, accuracy) series. After $T_1$ training it computes the scalar summaries of Section~\ref{subsubsec:gap_metrics}. A gradient tracker records, at the same per-step cadence, the directional and magnitude fidelity of the replay-buffer gradient against the deterministic empirical past-task gradient $g_{\text{true}}$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:grad_metrics}).

Periodic evaluation, every $10$ steps, is used for the general training-loss and final-accuracy curves outside the boundary window. The CIFAR-10 block of Section~\ref{subsec:cifar} coarsens the dense cadence to every $50$ steps over a $250$-step window: at ten epochs per task the post-switch dynamics play out over thousands of steps, so a step-$1$ cadence over a $500$-step window would no longer cover the gap and would inflate logging cost on the deeper backbone.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

All metrics are recorded automatically by the evaluation pipeline and reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation across the five seeds.

\label{subsubsec:gap_metrics}

These are computed from the dense per-step evaluation. Write $a_j^{\text{pre}}$ for the pre-task accuracy on task $j$ and $a_j(t)$ for its accuracy at step $t$ of the new task.

\subsubsection{Gradient-fidelity diagnostics}
\label{subsubsec:grad_metrics}

The gradient tracker compares the replay-buffer gradient $g_{\text{replay}}$ against the true past-task gradient $g_{\text{true}}$, computed at every step by a separate forward and backward pass over the entire past-task training set (no sub-sampling, so $g_{\text{true}}$ is the deterministic empirical past-task gradient at the current parameters). Table~\ref{tab:grad_metrics} lists the diagnostics.

\begin{table*}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{p{4.0cm} p{2.6cm} p{8.0cm}}
        \hline
        Diagnostic & Logged name & Role \\
        \hline
        $\cos(g_{\text{replay}}, g_{\text{true}})$ & \texttt{true\_grad\_cosine} & Buffer-side directional fidelity --- the estimator-bias contributor of Section~\ref{subsec:decomp} \\
        $\|g_{\text{replay}}\| / \|g_{\text{true}}\|$ & \texttt{true\_grad\_mag\_ratio} & Buffer-side magnitude fidelity \\
        $\|g_{\text{new}}\| / \|g_{\text{replay}}\|$ & \texttt{grad\_ratio} & Update-side magnitude asymmetry --- large at step $1$ when $\|g_{\text{replay}}\| \approx 0$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Gradient-fidelity diagnostics, recorded over the new task's first ${\sim}500$ steps. Each is logged as a per-step series together with its mean (and minimum, for the cosine).}
    \label{tab:grad_metrics}
\end{table*}

The first two diagnostics carry the \texttt{true\_grad} prefix because they are measured against the true full-data past-task gradient $g_{\text{true}}$: they quantify how faithfully a finite buffer approximates the past data the gradient is meant to represent \citep{aljundi2019gradientbasedsampleselection}. The third, \texttt{grad\_ratio}, compares $g_{\text{replay}}$ to the current-task gradient $g_{\text{new}}$ and is unrelated to $g_{\text{true}}$; its inverse $\|g_{\text{replay}}\| / \|g_{\text{new}}\|$ is the signal the adaptive curriculum of Section~\ref{subsec:curriculum} uses to set $\lambda(t)$.

The buffer-fidelity diagnostics are enabled for the decomposition conditions only (Section~\ref{subsec:decomp}), where they give direct per-step evidence for the estimator-bias contributor and pair with the indirect evidence from the depth difference between balanced and full-data conditions. Each step adds one forward and backward pass over every past-task training sample ($60{,}000$ for rot-MNIST $T_0$), which is affordable on the MLP testbed but would dominate runtime on larger backbones, so the toggle stays off for the curriculum blocks, whose claims rest on \texttt{gap\_depth} and ACC rather than per-step gradient geometry.

\subsubsection{Statistical comparison}

Across conditions we compare metrics with the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the seed-paired difference. Seeds are paired by index across conditions, so the per-seed deltas $\Delta_s = m(C_a, s) - m(C_b, s)$ factor out the seed-induced variance that would otherwise dominate the five-seed sample. We prefer Wilcoxon over the paired $t$-test because \texttt{gap\_depth} and accuracy are bounded in $[0,1]$ and typically right-skewed near zero, so the $t$-test's normality assumption is suspect; the Wilcoxon test exchanges that assumption for the milder one that $\Delta_s$ is distributionally symmetric under the null. For equivalence-style claims, such as ``the adaptive curriculum matches the best linear curriculum'', we report the seed-paired difference with its bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval rather than a null-hypothesis test.

\subsection{Three-Contributor Decomposition}
\label{subsec:decomp}

\paragraph{Aim.} Isolate the three contributors of Section~\ref{subsec:decomposition} --- magnitude asymmetry ($G_{\text{mag}}$), estimator variance ($G_{\text{est}}$), and the trajectory effect ($G_{\text{traj}}$) --- and quantify each one's share of the total gap. This is the diagnostic question of RQ5.

\paragraph{Rationale.} Two of the three contributors are predicted to be downstream symptoms of the discontinuity at the task boundary; the third, estimator variance, is a property of finite replay buffers and persists at any schedule. Removing each contributor by construction and observing the residual gap depth identifies which contributors drive the dip. The path G1 $\rightarrow$ G2 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4 removes one source at a time (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}).

\begin{table*}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{p{2.8cm} p{2.4cm} p{2.6cm} p{3.0cm} p{3.0cm}}
        \hline
        Condition & Replay buffer & Gradient handling & Removed by construction & Residual gap \\
        \hline
        G1 --- Vanilla ER & $1$k reservoir & unbalanced & nothing & $G_{\text{mag}}{+}G_{\text{est}}{+}G_{\text{traj}}$ \\
        G2 --- Full-data ER & $60$k (all past) & unbalanced & estimator noise (exact gradient) & $G_{\text{mag}}{+}G_{\text{traj}}$ \\
        G3 --- Balanced ER & $1$k reservoir & unit-norm balanced & magnitude asymmetry & $G_{\text{est}}{+}G_{\text{traj}}$ ({+}\,balancing cost) \\
        G4 --- Full-data + balanced & $60$k & unit-norm balanced & magnitude and estimator & $G_{\text{traj}}$ \\
        NCL (reference) & none & K-FAC precision preconditioning & --- & path-finding reference \citep{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess} \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Decomposition conditions. Each row removes one source of the gap relative to G1, so the residual gap shrinks down the path G1 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4.}
    \label{tab:decomp}
\end{table*}

\paragraph{Full-data conditions.} The full-data conditions G2 and G4 hold the entire past-task training set in the buffer and, at every step, compute the replay gradient on every stored sample exactly once. The resulting replay gradient is the deterministic empirical past-task gradient at the current parameters --- no sampling, no bootstrap variance. Vanilla ER (G1) draws $256$ samples with replacement, so its replay gradient is a stochastic estimator of this same quantity. The estimator contributor $G_{\text{est}}$ is therefore exactly the gap-depth difference between an estimator-driven step and an exact-gradient step, and $G_{\text{traj}}$ in G4 is the residual once both the estimator and the magnitude asymmetry are removed.

\paragraph{Decomposition shares.} Differences along the path identify each contributor:
\begin{align}
    G_{\text{mag}}  &:= \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G1}) - \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G3}), \\
    G_{\text{est}}  &:= \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G3}) - \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G4}), \\
    G_{\text{traj}} &:= \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G4}).
\end{align}
$G_{\text{mag}}$ is the share removed by balancing alone; $G_{\text{est}}$ is the additional share removed by full data on top of balancing; $G_{\text{traj}}$ is the residual after both are removed, the trajectory contribution of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The G2 condition is a sanity check: $\texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G1}) - \texttt{gap\_depth}(\text{G2})$ should recover $G_{\text{est}}$ along an orthogonal path through the design.

\paragraph{NCL reference.} NCL is included as a representative path-finding method (Section~\ref{subsec:pathfinding_vs_shaping}): it accepts the discontinuity but preconditions each SGD step by the past-task precision matrix. Its gap depth is the natural reference for how much depth reduction is achievable by acting in parameter space rather than in schedule space, and gives a fair comparison point for the $\lambda$-curriculum.

\paragraph{Expectation.} Gap depth reduces along the path G1 $\rightarrow$ G3 $\rightarrow$ G4. The trajectory residual at G4 is small but non-zero (the prediction of \citet{kao2021naturalcontinuallearningsuccess}, Section~\ref{subsec:stability_gap}), and is the contributor the $\lambda$-curriculum should additionally suppress.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda}-Curriculum Sweep}
\label{subsec:curriculum}

\paragraph{Aim.} Test the envelope-theorem prediction of Section~\ref{sec:theory} --- that a continuous $\lambda$-schedule eliminates the trajectory contribution to the gap, with a transient that shrinks as $O(1/N)$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}) --- and the $\lambda_{\min}$ trade-off. Answers RQ1, RQ2, and RQ4.

\paragraph{Conditions.} All curriculum conditions are applied on top of standard ER ($1$k reservoir, no balancing, no NCL) so the curriculum's effect is isolated (Table~\ref{tab:curriculum}).

\begin{table*}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{p{3.4cm} p{6.5cm} p{3.0cm} p{1.2cm}}
        \hline
        Condition & $\lambda(t)$ schedule & Hyperparameter & Tests \\
        \hline
        Vanilla ER (baseline) & $\lambda \equiv 1$ from step $1$ (discontinuous) & --- & reference \\
        Linear $N{=}50$ & $\mathrm{clip}(t/50,\, 0,\, 1)$ & $N = 50$ & RQ1 \\
        Linear $N{=}100$ & $\mathrm{clip}(t/100,\, 0,\, 1)$ & $N = 100$ & RQ1 \\
        Linear $N{=}200$ & $\mathrm{clip}(t/200,\, 0,\, 1)$ & $N = 200$ & RQ1 \\
        Adaptive & $\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\mathrm{EMA}_\alpha(\|g_{\text{replay}}\|/\|g_{\text{new}}\|),\, 0,\, 1\right)$ & $\alpha = 0.05$ & RQ2 \\
        Adaptive $+\,\lambda_{\min}$ & $\max\!\left(\lambda_{\min},\, \mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{EMA}_\alpha(\|g_{\text{replay}}\|/\|g_{\text{new}}\|),\, 0,\, 1)\right)$ & $\alpha = 0.05$, $\lambda_{\min} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$ & RQ4 \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{$\lambda$-curriculum conditions, all applied on top of standard ER. $\mathrm{EMA}_\alpha$ is an exponential moving average with smoothing $\alpha$.}
    \label{tab:curriculum}
\end{table*}

The adaptive schedule is self-paced. At $\theta_0^*$ the replay gradient is near zero by stationarity, so the ratio $r := \|g_{\text{replay}}\| / \|g_{\text{new}}\|$ is near zero and $\lambda$ starts near $0$. As the iterate leaves $\theta_0^*$, $\|g_{\text{replay}}\|$ grows while $\|g_{\text{new}}\|$ shrinks as the model fits $T_1$, so $r$ climbs and $\lambda \to 1$ with no explicit ramp length; the schedule ignores $N$ entirely. The floor $\lambda_{\min}$ matters mainly for this adaptive variant: it stops the schedule from sitting at $\lambda \approx 0$ in the first steps, when the EMA estimate of $r$ is still close to zero.

\paragraph{Expectation.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Linear sweep} (RQ1). \texttt{gap\_depth} should fall monotonically as $N$ grows from $50$ to $200$, consistent with the $O(1/N)$ bound of Equation~\eqref{eq:bound}. ACC should stay close to vanilla ER, because the curriculum preserves SGD's natural step-size adaptation rather than rescaling the step the way gradient balancing does.
    \item \emph{Adaptive} (RQ2). The adaptive schedule should achieve a depth reduction comparable to a tuned linear curriculum, removing the need to fix $N$ as a hyperparameter.
    \item \emph{$\lambda_{\min}$} (RQ4). Raising $\lambda_{\min}$ from $0$ should lift the gap floor slightly --- the trajectory cannot dip below the replay loss at the floor minimiser --- while accelerating $T_1$ learning. We expect a small $\lambda_{\min} \in [0.05, 0.15]$ to give a better Pareto position on the (depth, ACC) trade-off than $\lambda_{\min} = 0$.
\end{itemize}

The contrast between balanced ER (G3) and linear $N{=}200$ isolates the difference between acting on the magnitude symptom alone and acting on the schedule discontinuity: both should suppress depth, but only the curriculum should preserve ACC.

\subsection{Momentum and the Curriculum \texorpdfstring{$\times$}{x} Momentum Cross}
\label{subsec:momentum}

\paragraph{Design.} Every rot-MNIST condition of Sections~\ref{subsec:decomp} and~\ref{subsec:curriculum} is repeated with momentum $0.9$ (Nesterov off), giving a $2 \times (\text{decomposition} + \text{curriculum})$ cross (Table~\ref{tab:momentum}). \subsection{Generalisation: CIFAR-10}
\label{subsec:cifar}

\paragraph{Aim.} Confirm the direction of the rot-MNIST findings under a stronger task shift and a deeper, harder-to-analyse architecture --- the regime in which the assumptions behind the envelope-theorem bound weaken (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).

\paragraph{Dataset.} A two-task domain-shift CIFAR-10: $T_0$ uses clean CIFAR-10 and $T_1$ uses the Gaussian-noise corruption at severity $3$. Both tasks share the same ten classes, so only the input distribution shifts. As in rot-MNIST, two tasks produce exactly one transition.

\paragraph{Training protocol.} A CIFAR-adapted ResNet-18 ($3\times3$ stem, no max-pool stem; see Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}), ten epochs per task --- at this depth ResNet-18 needs more than one pass to converge before the transition --- batch size $256$, SGD with learning rate $0.1$, and momentum $0.9$ only. The momentum cross of Section~\ref{subsec:momentum} is not repeated; momentum $0.9$ is locked in as the better-performing leg from rot-MNIST. The dense evaluation cadence is coarsened to every $50$ steps over a $250$-step window (Section~\ref{subsubsec:eval_protocol}).

\paragraph{Conditions.} Four headline conditions only, not the full sweep (Table~\ref{tab:cifar}). A linear curriculum is not run on CIFAR; only the two best-performing adaptive variants from the rot-MNIST $\lambda_{\min}$ refinement are carried over, alongside the two reference points. NCL hyperparameters are pinned to the values that won the rot-MNIST tuning sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}).
